Settled 2026-08-30: synthetic data, illustrating continuous and discrete likelihoods together (Kenneth's pick), and both acts in scope. Hosts at PHM5005 04-3 Tour of Algorithms § Probabilistic — Arc A slot 5. The claim: P(y|x) ∝ P(y) · Π P(xⱼ|y) — a prior times one likelihood per feature, whatever shape each likelihood takes (Gaussian for a lab, Bernoulli for a symptom). Act 1 scores ONE patient feature by feature; the model is fitted live on a seeded cohort (n 400, prevalence 0.3, two labs, two symptoms), and the default patient is the conflicted one — evidence pulls both ways and the posterior lands near ½. Act 2 is the caveat the notebook names: correlated features double-count. Design numbers in _lab/nb-design.py — the mixed Gaussian×Bernoulli arithmetic matches sklearn to 5e-16, and the ρ sweep only tells the double-counting story when the second lab is redundant (matched standardized shifts); mismatched shifts send the CORRECT posterior to 1.0 instead, the opposite lesson.

§2 · Act 2 — the naive assumption double-counts

Two labs with matched standardized shifts, a ρ dial, population parameters. Left: the cohort tilts as ρ rises while naive Bayes's model of it — the axis-aligned ellipses, built from marginals alone — cannot tilt. Right: the two posteriors for the elevated patient as ρ moves. Naive Bayes never moves (its inputs are the marginals, and they never change); the correct model relaxes to the one-lab answer as the second lab stops adding information. At ρ 0.95: NB 0.845 against correct 0.613, the one-lab answer being 0.605.
