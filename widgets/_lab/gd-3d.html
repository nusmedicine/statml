Widget 48 · gradient-descent — the surface in relief, as an option

Kenneth's question (2026-09-07): could the surface be shown in 3D? Drawn here with no library — a mesh of the loss over (b₀, b₁) projected onto the canvas, painted back to front, lit from one side, the path laid on it and the two partial derivatives drawn as the tangent segments along each axis at the current point, which is what a partial derivative is. The engine is the widget's own model.js; the walk is the same walk.

The design problem is the height. The loss spans ~270× from the start to the least, and the trench floor sits at 1–8×. Linear height is a cliff over a flat floor and the walk along the trench is invisible; a log height turns the trench into a canyon whose floor still slopes. §1 shows the three mappings; §2 the candidates at the widget's own panel size; §3 the round bowl of standardized x. Rotate with the rail — a fixed viewpoint or a display control for a few is the pick; a drag would be a region and a hit-driven state.

§1 · The height mapping — raw x, the lesson's own surface

linear: height ∝ loss, capped at 30× the least (the cap is the plateau); square root: height ∝ √(loss ÷ least), capped at 316×; log: height ∝ log₁₀(loss ÷ least), the same mapping the 2D surface's colour uses, so the contour rings sit at equal heights.

§2 · Candidates at the widget's panel size (log height)

A shaded relief with the contour rings and the path — the 2D map's colours, lifted; B the relief with the two partial derivatives as tangent segments at the current point, and the composed downhill direction; C a wireframe with the path only — lightest, and the floor is hard to read without shading. All three at 240px, the surface's side at the 550px fingerprint width, then A again at 300px, the side at the widest layout.

What it would cost in the widget

Occlusion. A path behind a ridge is hidden unless drawn on top of the mesh, which then floats where it should be behind; the viewpoints here are chosen so the start (0, 0) and the trench face the camera, and a fixed viewpoint is what makes that hold. The component ticks (pick §2 C) become tangent segments on the surface, which is a better drawing of a partial derivative than the map's ticks — but the composed direction lies in the tangent plane and reads only from the side. Height. The panel's square is shared between the plan and the relief, so the mesh gets ~240px in which the canyon's floor is a few pixels deep; nothing here adds height to the widget. Cost. A 48×48 mesh is 2304 quads painted per frame; at Fast that is 250 epochs a second of a cached mesh with only the path and the point redrawn, so the mesh must be painted once to a bitmap per viewpoint, as the 2D surface already is. The state. A relief display switch on the two-parameter page, off by default, keeps the map the record and the relief a reading of it.
