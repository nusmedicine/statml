2 · Sparse: fewer active units

The embedding of 50 test digits (5 of each, rows grouped by digit) across the 16 units (columns), ink at the value, a 0 left blank. The notebook draws this as a clustermap (cell 52) and a histogram of the values (cell 50).

The notebook's sparse autoencoder has 9 dead units, not a sparse code. Over all 10,000 test digits, 9 of its 16 units are 0 for every digit (they stop responding within the first 5 batches; 2 are already 0 at random weights), and the other 7 are active on 98% of digits. The 57% of zeros are those 9 columns. The L1 penalty is not the cause and changes nothing measurable: the same network with λ = 0 has the same 9 dead units, MSE 0.022 against 0.021, 5-NN 91.0% against 91.5%. A ReLU placed on a small bottleneck switches units off for good (the "dying ReLU"). Every ReLU setting tried did the same (16 or 64 units, λ 0 to 0.01, learning rate 10−4: 6 to 18 live units, each active on nearly every digit).
No simple setting gave a sparse code. The textbook alternatives were tried too: a KL penalty on a sigmoid code (64 units, ρ = 0.05) meets its target by keeping every unit near 0.05 for every digit (largest value 0.18), so nothing switches off; keeping each image's top 8 of 64 units leaves 54 units unused, all digits sharing the same few. A code where each digit uses its own few units needs more tuning than a setting change.

Recommend D, drop the Sparse page: as 10-1b stands its sparsity is the 9 dead units, which the page would have to say, and that is a finding about the notebook rather than a lesson. A keeps it and says so, with the grid beside the autoencoder's (the blank columns are the dead units). I can look further for a setting that gives a real sparse code if you want the section kept as a lesson; that is a measurement, not a page.

3 · Contrastive: the views, and the steps

The views come from the notebook's augmentation (cell 74): RandomCrop(28, padding=4) shifts the digit up to 4 pixels, then RandomAffine(15, translate=(0.1, 0.1), scale=(0.9, 1.1)) rotates it up to 15°, shifts it up to 2.8 more pixels and scales it by up to 10%. On a centred MNIST digit that is a small change, which is why the two views look alike. Below: the views overlaid, the matrix with its axes drawn as the images, and the chosen digit's row as the softmax InfoNCE scores.

Recommend all three on the page, in the order of the steps: 1 Augment (the two views overlaid, with each view's shift, angle and scale), 2 Compare (the matrix, rows view 1 and columns view 2, each labelled by its image), 3 Loss (the chosen row's softmax: the bar on its matching view is the probability the loss rewards; L = −log of it). Train then plays the checkpoints through the matrix and the row.
