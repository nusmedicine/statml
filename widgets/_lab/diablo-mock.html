0 · What the measurement found

1 · DIABLO's loop is 99's loop with the subtype added as one more block. mixOmics' sparse.mint.block_iteration: each block's loading vector is Xᵀz, where z is the other blocks' scores weighted by the design matrix; the subtype (Y, as an indicator matrix) is a block linked to every omics block at 1 (03 cell 16 prints "Design matrix has changed to include Y"). Then the vector is cut to its keepX largest entries, and the next block updates. The loop starts each block at its own direction of largest variance (init = "svd.single"), which is 99's dashed benchmark.
2 · The design weight trades separating the subtypes against agreeing, and agreement barely moves. On the lesson's blocks, weight 0 → 1 takes the mRNA–methylation agreement 0.849 → 0.868 and the CV error 0.195 → 0.256 (4 components); component 1's subtype share in mRNA 0.75 → 0.63. The blocks already agree through the subtype they are all fitted to. The example blocks reproduce this: r 0.95 → 0.98, subtype share 0.51 → 0.17, the arrow turning 35°.
3 · With three blocks the vote helps; with RPPA missing it is mRNA's call. 10-fold CV on the 405 (one repeat, the lesson's keepX and design 0.1): the vote's BER at 4 components is 0.232 against 0.242 · 0.295 · 0.355 for mRNA, methylation and RPPA alone, overruling mRNA on 12–33 samples. With two blocks a weighted vote goes to the heavier block on every disagreement, and mRNA's weight (0.62) beats methylation's (0.60): on the 102 test samples the vote equals mRNA's call 102 of 102 times at every number of components, including where methylation alone scores better (test BER at 4 components: mRNA 0.304, methylation 0.280, vote 0.304).
4 · keepX keeps a component, from few genes. Component 1 of mRNA from 25 genes correlates 0.95 with component 1 from all 8,383 and separates the subtypes better (share 0.72 against 0.66). The 25 are not simply the top 25 of the full loading vector: 16 are, and the rest come from ranks up to 58, because the cut happens inside every update and changes the other blocks' scores too. CV BER at 4 components, same folds: 0.232 with keepX, 0.274 with every feature (keepX was tuned on these samples, so the first is optimistic; 101's subject).
Notebook finding 6 · 05 cell 6's table is not the final model's CV. It loads 03's perf.diablo.ucec.rds, which is perf on 03 cell 16's block.plsda with 5 components and every feature (its columns run comp1–comp5; the final model has 4). 05 cell 3 shows the perf(diablo.ucec, …) call as if run on the sparse model.
Notebook finding 7 · 05 cells 21–24's prediction is mRNA's alone. The warning says it is "based on mRNA, meth"; with two blocks the weighted vote returns the heavier block's call on all 102 samples.

99's two blocks rebuilt for this question: they share a large axis that has nothing to do with the subtype (as the lesson's latent variable 3 does: r 0.85, subtype share 0.02), and each holds the subtype on an axis of its own. Presses: Start (each arrow on its block's direction of largest variance, mixOmics' start) · Update mRNA · Update methylation · … until the arrows stop (2–6 rounds by weight). Each update's arithmetic is the middle panel: z = c·u + 1·y, then w ∝ Xᵀz, so the weight c sets how much the arrow follows the other block against the subtype.
