Rotating the cloud, and how many groups

Two questions that need eyes rather than argument: does dragging the cloud around actually help you read it, and how many groups still look like groups at the real width. Drag on the figure to turn it; Default view puts it back.

One measurement settled a third question before this page was drawn. The group centres are laid out in a plane, not along a line, and that is what makes the second component worth having: with centres on a line PC1 alone already separates everything and PC2 is noise. In a plane, PC1 alone gets worse as you add groups — 100% at two, 51% at six — while PC1 and PC2 stay at 100%. The table under the figure is that, live.

Gated, or just Play?

Not gated. A gate is for a stage the reader has to choose to enter — widget 18 has three, and each one is a decision with a consequence. The four beats here are one continuous story with no decision in it, so three gates would add three presses that mean nothing and give the reader three chances to stop halfway. Step plus Play is the grammar, with the readout naming which of the four you are in. Step is there for a lecturer talking over it; Play is there for a student who wants the story. That is principle 4.1 — pacing is chosen, and this chooses it.
