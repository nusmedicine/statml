0 · How others draw this (research, 2026-10-09)

  • Distill, A Gentle Introduction to GNNs (Sanchez-Lengeling et al. 2021): hover a node to see which edges are gathered and pooled into it; one layer is "graph in, graph out" with the same connectivity. Stacking and the receptive field are stated in prose (three layers, three steps away), not animated.
  • Distill, Understanding Convolutions on Graphs (Daigavane et al. 2021): click a node to see its update equation, sliders for the weights; the neighbourhood figure shows one hop only; K hops are prose again.
  • CS224W (Leskovec), lectures 5–6: the computation graph — a node's K layers unrolled as a tree from its neighbourhood, depth = layers; two nodes with the same tree get the same embedding (the point Readout makes).
  • Kipf & Welling 2017, Figure 1: the network drawn as copies of the graph, input layer → hidden layers → output layer — the stacked-sheets picture you described.

So nobody I found animates the stack or the receptive field growing; the two static conventions are the unrolled tree (CS224W) and stacked graph copies (Kipf & Welling). Both are drawn below, animated, beside a flat cloud.

1 · Transform — making h′ arrive (09-1 cell 23: "then Transform")

Each arm starts after Aggregate (every node holds its sum) and has one press, Transform. The point to carry: one W for every node (the same weights, as a CNN's kernel is the same at every pixel), then the activation.

Arm C. The three sums drop into one stack (H), a highlight sweeps W's columns while H·W fills a column at a time, the result slides through the ELU gate (negative values bend to just above −1), and each new row rises back to its own node. Every motion has its own lane, so nothing crosses. It is about 5 s; the caffeine page would keep the table close-up (B) since 14 rows do not fit a stack.
