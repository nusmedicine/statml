77 count-normalization · genes and reads, as the HBC page draws them · mock

His ask, 2026-09-21: could we have similar illustrations of genes and reads like the hbctraining website? also the color of the legends is confusing. That page draws a gene as a bar of its length and each read as a small rectangle stacked above it, Sample A over Sample B. Below, the same six genes as before drawn that way, with counts small enough to count: the number of rectangles is the raw count; the depth of the pile is the coverage, which is what a per-kilobase unit (FPKM, TPM) reads and a per-million unit (CPM) does not. Every number is computed on this page. Drawn at the widget's 550px; the ratio over each gene is B ÷ A in the unit named, as the widget prints it.

1 · The three steps as pile-ups (legend scheme A: one hue for reads, samples are rows)

a read a read of a gene that changed the gene, drawn to its length

Depth: equal lengths, B sequenced 3× deeper — every pile is three times as deep, every raw ratio 3.00. Length: every gene at one expression per kilobase, lengths differ — the piles are all the same depth, and the long gene has many more rectangles: raw and CPM count rectangles, FPKM and TPM read depth. Composition: the same number of reads in each sample (the sequencer's capacity), gene 5 up 8× in B — it takes most of B's rectangles and every other gene's pile thins, though nothing else changed. The size factor reads the five thinned piles as the unchanged ones and rescales B by their median.

Recommended: this replaces the paired bars on all three steps. It is the page's own picture, and it shows the thing the bars asserted — that a count is rectangles and a per-kilobase unit is depth — and on Composition it shows the reads themselves being taken. Composition changes to a FIXED total of reads per sample, which is the HBC framing and the true one (a sequencer has a capacity), so the unchanged genes' raw counts fall and the reader sees why.

2 · The legend: scheme A against scheme B, on the Composition step in TPM

The confusion: Sample A's blue and the scatter's blue dots were the same hue for two different things, and Sample B's yellow outline read as a third series. A — one hue for reads and for the scatter's genes (both are data), the highlight for a gene that changed, samples told apart by their ROW and their label, never by colour. B — Sample A and Sample B in the two group hues on the piles, the scatter's genes in a third hue.

Recommended A: three legend entries instead of five, and blue means one thing everywhere (a gene's data). A sample is a row with a name, which is what a sample is.

The pile-up says why. The scatter says by how much over 2,000 genes (the median shift, the count called down at 1.5×) and is where the share control's failing case shows. Left, the pile-up alone with the numbers in tiles; right, the pile-up over the scatter as the widget has it now.
