§H · The enclosure, live — and what “outside it” means

Your first figure with one enclosure instead of seven. Click a token to move it in or out. A token outside keeps its slot and starts taking values from other patients; each substitution paints one tick on the strip and the running mean walks along. The printed number is the mean of the ticks actually on screen, so it is true at every frame and exact when the deal finishes.

§J · Your second figure, animated — and the average it stops short of

Your four panels are the four distinct contributions of one player: v({1}), v({1,2})−v({2}), v({1,3})−v({3}), v({1,2,3})−v({2,3}). Here the token leaves each group and the payoff drops by that much, one panel at a time — then the four drops are collected into the tray, at the weights the formula gives them, and φ is where they balance.

§K · The queue — the same tokens, arriving in an order

The tokens wait outside and walk in one at a time. Each arrival steps the payoff, and the step is recorded. Then the queue reshuffles and it runs again: six queues, six rows, and the Shapley value is the column average. Switch the model to the notebook's logistic regression and every row comes out identical — which is the one thing about SHAP that the notebook's own example can never show.
