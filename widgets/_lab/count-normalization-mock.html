3 · The failing case: the median of ratios past half the genes

The size factor assumes most genes are unchanged. The Composition page's share control runs it out: the median log2(B / A) of the UNCHANGED genes under TPM and under the size factor as the share of genes moving up rises, at 2× and 8×, and when the movers go both ways.

The share control's top values are where the widget includes the case that fails (2.6): at half the genes up 8× the size factor reads the unchanged genes down 1.6 log2, the same call TPM makes. When the movers go both ways it holds to half. The readout should print both numbers.

4 · The size factor: a gated walkthrough, or the number alone

39 normalization gates its quantile walkthrough behind a button because quantile is the one method with no closed form. The median of ratios has three steps and the notebook prints them: the geometric mean per gene, each sample's ratio to it, the median per sample. Left, the walkthrough as a table on the six genes; right, the number alone in a readout tile.

Recommended the walkthrough, gated, on the Composition page under the Size factor option, as 39's is under Quantile: the reader sees the changed gene's ratio sit off to one side and the median step past it, which is the whole argument for why the method survives a composition change and a share does not.

5 · What the count carries that the unit does not

Cell 16's reason DESeq2 wants counts. A TPM of 50 is 50 reads at 1M depth and 2,500 at 50M; the Poisson CV is 14% against 2%. Three places it could go: a readout tile on the Depth page; a fourth page; a caption on the Depth page.

Recommended a tile on the Depth page, since the depth control is the one that moves it: raise B's depth and the tile shows the same TPM carrying more reads. A fourth page is a page for one sentence.

6 · Subtitle candidates (2.10: two or three claims that read written)

S1 A read count depends on the sample's depth and the gene's length as well as its expression. CPM, FPKM and TPM divide these out, but each is a share of the sample's total, so when some genes rise every other gene's share falls. A size factor takes the scale from the genes that did not change.
S2 Counts differ between samples by depth and between genes by length. A unit that divides both out is a share of the sample, and a share moves when other genes move; the size factor is read from the genes that stay put, so it does not.
S3 Raw counts carry sequencing depth and gene length. CPM removes depth, FPKM and TPM remove both, and all three are shares of one sample's reads, which a change in a few genes shifts for every gene. DESeq2's size factor is the median ratio over genes, most of which are assumed unchanged.

Recommended S1: concept first, mechanism second, no vocabulary the rail does not also carry. Blurb (under 120): Depth, length and composition move a count; CPM, FPKM and TPM divide out the first two, and a size factor survives the third.
