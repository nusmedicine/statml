NMDS — in widget 20, or a widget of its own

THE LESSON ANSWERS THE STRUCTURAL HALF. PHM5003 05 · Introduction to High Throughput Data / 04 · Dimensionality Reduction has one section — ## 3. Multidimensional Scaling (MDS): Classical and Non-metric — with ### 1) Classical MDS and ### 2) Non-metric MDS under it, on the same distMatrix, one after the other. One topic, so one widget. What is still open is what NMDS shows, and that is what everything below is for.

Every number on this page is computed here, from the widget’s own stage and its own solver. The default is two groups of four, which is the lesson’s design: the airway dataset is 4 controls and 4 dexamethasone-treated samples, and it runs cmdscale(dist(t(countData)), k = 2) then isoMDS(distMatrix, k = 2). Widget 20 caps samples-per-group at 3, so it cannot currently reproduce that design — adding a fourth option is one line and the table holds it at 13px wide / 11px narrow.

1 · The whole widget, with NMDS in it

The 2×2, at the real width

NMDS continues from where MDS stopped, which is not a convenience: isoMDS’s default starting configuration is cmdscale, so “one after another” is what the library itself does. It also matters numerically — from a random start, non-metric MDS collapsed 18 of 40 seeds into degenerate clumps at eight samples. From the metric fit it never did. Press through the stages in order.

Seed 12, and it is picked rather than default. Seed 1 fits so well that the rank-order stage converges in one step and shows nothing; 12 recovers 3.2× the cluster separation against a median of 1.7× over 40 seeds, and runs 59 steps rather than hitting the cap. Section 5 is the honest typical case — this one is picked to be legible.

3 · What the table says once the fit is non-metric

Three treatments, at the real cell size

This is the sharp problem. The widget’s argument is two numbers per cell — the distance the samples have, and under it the distance the picture managed — and NMDS is not trying to make the second equal the first. It is matching f(D), a monotone transform of the first, refitted at every step. So the second number stops being comparable to what is above it.

4 · The transform, which is the lesson’s own diagram

Fitted distance against measured dissimilarity

The lesson carries a figure called nmds-transform.png for exactly this. Metric MDS is trying to put the points on the diagonal; non-metric MDS is trying to put them on any rising line, and the staircase is the f it fitted. It is the only place the difference between the two methods is visible as a shape rather than as a number — which is the argument for finding it a fifth panel, and the argument against is that the widget has four already.

5 · What the numbers say

Eight samples, two groups of four, 40 seeds, medians

Squashed raises every dissimilarity to the power 0.35 — large ones compressed toward each other, rank order untouched. That is the lesson’s “non-Euclidean distances” case, and it is the only condition under which the two methods disagree about the picture.

Separation is the smallest between-cluster distance over the largest within-cluster one: above 1 means the clusters are still distinct blobs.

6 · What this settles, and what it does not

ONE WIDGET, as a third gate — the lesson puts both under one heading on one distance matrix, isoMDS starts from cmdscale, and the sequence is what stops NMDS degenerating. Three things need your call:

1. The table. Treatment 3 (ranks) is the only one that stays honest under NMDS, because it shows what is actually being matched — but it means the table changes what it prints when the second gate opens, and the reader has to notice.
2. The transform panel. It is the lesson’s own figure and the only visible difference between the methods, but the 2×2 is full and it would make the widget 2×3.
3. The stress number changes meaning. Widget 20 prints RAW stress, matching sklearn’s stress_ and the lesson’s classical formula. The lesson’s NMDS formula is normalised, and the two are not comparable — so putting both on one chart forces the widget onto Kruskal stress-1 throughout, and every stress figure recorded so far changes.

And whether the squash control earns its place. Without it NMDS lands in the same picture as MDS with a lower stress, which is true and undersells it; with it, metric MDS visibly loses the clusters and NMDS gets them back. It is a fourth control on a rail that has three.
