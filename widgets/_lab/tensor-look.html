tensors · colour, type size, and motion

Three questions after round 8, each measured before it is drawn. Colour: the cells are washes of the series colours with the value printed in ink; the contrast ratios below are computed from tokens.css for the theme this page is in. Type: the stage is 550px wide in the fingerprint frame and about 770 in a wide window; projected full-screen at 1080p the canvas is about 1.4× its 550px size, and the presentation rule of thumb is that the smallest text should be 1/50 of the screen height — 21.6px on a 1080p projector, so about 15px on the 550px stage. Motion: principle 4.4 allows an ease only where two settings are two readings of the SAME data; Heer and Robertson (2007) found that animated transitions aid object constancy and that complex transitions are better STAGED than played at once; Material and NN/g put a standard transition at 200–300ms, ease-out for things arriving, ease-in-out for things moving.

§1 · Colour — the same state four ways, with the contrast measured

A as built: a blue wash at 0.20 under ink, the selection a violet wash at 0.34, values already moved in muted ink. B a stronger selection: violet at 0.50 with a 2px outline and bold digits; muted values lifted from --ink-3 to --ink-2. C solid fills with white digits, the way the lesson's own figures colour cells — and the numbers say why not: white on solid blue is 4.4:1 in the light theme and 3.6:1 in the dark, below the 4.5:1 text floor. D the dimensions carry a hue each on their labels and indices — dim 0, 1, 2 in the cluster hues — with the cells left neutral; the lesson's reshape figure colours its shape this way. The palette validator (dataviz skill) passes the eight series in both themes on lightness, chroma, colour-vision separation and normal-vision separation; in the light theme it WARNs that aqua, yellow and magenta sit below 3:1 against the surface as marks, which is why a yellow operand cell keeps its value in ink rather than in yellow.

§2 · Type — three sizes of the same Tensor band at 550px

Cell digits, the print, and the roles line at the built sizes and two steps up. A digit needs a cell about 1.8× its size to keep the number off the cell's edge, so bigger type means bigger cells, and the fit decides whether the print still sits beside the drawing. The table under the panels converts each size to what a back row sees on a 1080p projector.

§3 · Motion — press to play; the two transitions the Basics stage could earn

a · the view morph: switching Stack to Frames moves the SAME twenty cells to new places, so an ease shows they are the same tensor (object constancy) where a jump can only assert it — 4.4's one allowed case. Cells travel on ease-in-out; nothing appears or vanishes. b · the index extraction: choosing an index lights the selected cells (150ms), then copies of them glide down into the Index band and settle as the sub-tensor (300ms, ease-out) — staged, because the two changes are two ideas and Heer and Robertson found staging is what lets a reader follow both. c · the same extraction unstaged, for comparison: light and glide at once. A rank change is a DATA change and stays a jump; prefers-reduced-motion turns every ease into a jump, as core already does for Step.
