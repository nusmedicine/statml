2 · A schematic of the network

Not all three are autoencoders. They share an encoder (input → embedding z); what differs is the part attached to it during training and the target the loss compares against. The autoencoder adds a decoder that rebuilds the input; masked prediction adds one that predicts only the hidden part (with images, a masked autoencoder; with text, BERT's token prediction); contrastive learning has no decoder: the same encoder runs on two views, a projection head maps each embedding, and the loss compares the two. Any encoder can be trained this way: a CNN here; for sequences a transformer, for graphs a GNN, for tables an MLP (10-1b's own tables). After training the encoder is kept and the head is dropped.

Recommend A: a schematic row above the strip on each page, the encoder drawn the same on all three (the part that is kept) and the page's head and target beside it, with one line under it: "Encoder: any network that maps an input to a vector (here a CNN). After training the encoder is kept; the head is dropped." It adds about 92 px to each page. B costs no height: the strip's arrows are labelled with the network's parts, but the contrastive page's shared weights and the dropped head are harder to say there.
