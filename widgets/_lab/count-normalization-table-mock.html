"RPKM and TPM represent the relative abundance of a transcript among a population of sequenced transcripts, and therefore depend on the composition of the RNA population in a sample"

The common device is a small table of genes × samples where a within-sample comparison is reading DOWN a column and a between-sample comparison is reading ACROSS a row, with the column totals printed. Three steps that each isolate one confound were my shape, not the sources', and they hide the point: no unit gets both comparisons right, which is why a DE test takes counts and a size factor, and a heat map or a gene-against-gene look takes TPM.

Opens with all three on (3×, Differ, gene 5 up) and raw counts: every confound at once, and the reader's move is the unit. Turning one off isolates it, which is what the steps did, but by the reader's hand. The share-of-genes, fold and direction controls go: the failing case of the size factor (past half the genes moving) becomes a note under its table, and the 2,000-gene panel loses the reason it needed a share slider.

Recommended, as he proposed: one stage. It removes the Step control, the two rows of the pile-up that meant different things on different steps, and a control block per step.

2 · The table as the figure's middle: within is a column, between is a row

Six genes as rows; columns are the length, sample A and sample B in the chosen unit; a totals row. The within comparison is bracketed down column A: gene 5 against gene 6, which have the same expression per kilobase, so an honest unit reads 1.00. The between comparison is bracketed across gene 1's row: unchanged, so an honest unit reads 1.00. Both brackets end in a number, and the two numbers are the two tiles. Three units at the same data (3×, lengths differ, gene 5 up 8×):

This is the tile he asked for, as a picture first: Within sample A and Between samples, each with its number and the note 1.00 is right. Raw gets neither; CPM gets neither (depth yes, length no, composition no); FPKM and TPM get within; the size factor gets between. No unit gets both.

3 · The 2,000 genes as a histogram of ratios, replacing the scatter

The scatter showed a cloud off a diagonal, which is hard to read. The HBC page's own DESeq2 figure is a histogram of each gene's ratio between samples with the median marked, and that reads at once: where does the bulk of unchanged genes sit? At 0 is right. log2(B ÷ A) over 2,000 genes in the chosen unit; the changed genes are the bump in the highlight; the dashed line is the median over all genes, which is the size factor's own number.

Recommended: the histogram replaces the scatter. Under the size factor the bulk sits at 0 by construction and the bump stays where it is; under TPM the bulk is dragged left; under raw counts at 3× the bulk sits at log2 3 = 1.58. The tile beside it is the bulk's median, and 0 is right.
