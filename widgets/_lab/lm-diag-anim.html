Widget 33 · the residual animations, RUNNING — candidates for Kenneth's pick

Round 2's two asks, drawn as working animations on the widget's own generator (seed 6, n = 600): how the residual plot is built from the data, and why the residuals should be normal. Established patterns found in the wild and borrowed from: Whitlock's Visualizing residuals (click a point — its residual lights in both plots), the Regressomatic (data beside diagnostic, changes propagate), and the marginal-histogram edge panel (ggMarginal). The classic textbook diagram — sideways normal curves straddling the fitted line — seems never to have been animated; §C/§D are that. One naming note: the residual is the VERTICAL distance to the line, not the perpendicular — perpendicular distance is a different quantity (total least squares) — so every segment here is drawn vertical.

§A · Build the residual plot — selected residuals travel

Twelve selected patients (spread across BMI, the largest residual among them), one at a time: the vertical segment grows from the dot to the fitted line, then dot-and-segment TRAVEL into the lower panel to land at (fitted, residual) on the zero line. The remaining 588 fade in at the end. The residual plot is seen to BE the data plot with the line laid flat. This would run as the gate's entry animation (core's door), with Replay available.

candidate integration: the gate-open entry; afterwards hover/click a dot to light its residual in both panels (the Whitlock move — the arc's pointer channel)

§B · Lay the line flat — the whole cloud morphs

The alternative in one move, no selection: press the toggle and the fitted line rotates flat to zero while every dot carries its residual with it — the data panel BECOMES the residual plot (for one covariate the two share an x-axis). Cheaper than §A, covers all 600 dots, but nothing is singled out and the two-panel link is gone.

candidate integration: a segmented Data / Residuals view on the data panel (the 26/28 slide), with the two lower panels unchanged

§C · Why normal? — the residuals sweep into a sideways pile

The residual plot's dots sweep to the right edge and STACK into a sideways pile; the bell drawn over it (its width the residual SD) is what the normality assumption claims the pile should look like. On the skewed scenario the pile leans away from the bell — the thing the Q-Q then measures point by point. Toggle the scenario, replay the sweep.

candidate integration: an overlay toggle on the RvF panel ("Pile the residuals"), or the bridge beat between RvF and the Q-Q

§D · The model's claim, drawn on the data — bells straddle the line

The textbook diagram, live: at three BMI values a sideways normal curve straddles the fitted line — the model's claim that at EVERY x the outcome is normal around the line with ONE spread. The bells fade in with a stagger. On Unequal spread the fixed-width bells visibly disagree with the widening cloud; on Skewed noise the cloud leans out of them. This is the WHY the diagnostics check against, drawn where the data is.

candidate integration: a "Model's claim" overlay on the data panel — display toggle, no clock needed (or a one-off stagger on toggle)
