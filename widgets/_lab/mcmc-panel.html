What the MCMC tab should show — and how the sample arrives

Widget 9 currently shows a chain converging on a contour. That is true and it is the least interesting fact about it. MCMC does not compute the normalising constant — it avoids it, because P(counts) is the same number on the top and bottom of the acceptance ratio and cancels. That is the entire reason the method exists, and it is the misconception this tab has to dissolve. So the question is whether the cancellation can be seen, and what that costs in panel height.

The three things the tab could carry

The walk
a chain over the exact posterior. Already built. Shows that the draws pile up where the mass is, and that a Markov chain is not independent sampling.
The ratio
two bars — prior×L where the chain is, and where it wants to go — with the uniform draw as a dart thrown at the shorter one. Both bars would be divided by the same P(counts), so the comparison cannot change. This is the panel that fixes the misconception.
The result
a histogram of the draws with the exact marginal over it. It is the notebook's own figure (post_samples %>% ggplot(aes(x=mu)) + geom_histogram()) and it is the proof the chain recovers what the grid computed.

The ratio panel's scaling is the design problem. Metropolis ratios span orders of magnitude, so two bars on a shared linear scale would often be one full bar and nothing. These are drawn with the CURRENT point's height as full width, so the proposal's bar length is the acceptance probability, and u is a tick on the same scale. A proposal that is better than where you are runs past the line and is taken every time.

  1. Does the cancellation read? That is the whole test. Look at candidate B without reading the caption: are you looking at two things being compared, or two things being measured? If it reads as measurement, the panel has not earned its height.
  2. Is u legible as a dart? The acceptance rule is "draw a random number, keep it if it lands inside the shorter bar". If the tick reads as a scale mark rather than as a throw, it needs a different mark.
  3. In C, does the histogram add or dilute? It is the notebook's own figure and it proves the chain works — but it is a third idea in one panel, and the tab already has the walk and the ratio.
  4. Heights are printed under each. The marginal tabs are 636 px of canvas. A plane tab at 494 has room; one at 640 does not, and would make the widget's tallest state taller still.
  5. Flip between accept and reject. On a rejection the chain does not move, and the figure has to say that something happened anyway — otherwise a press looks like a no-op.
  6. Would you cut the walk? The honest question. If the ratio panel plus the histogram carries the lesson, the plane is decoration and the tab could be much shorter.

And separately: how the fixed sample should arrive

The counts are drawn once and never redrawn — that is already true, because compute() is pure and seeded — but the figure does not say so. With a lead action ("Draw the counts"), all twelve land at once and Step then moves them one at a time from pending to observed. Three ways to draw the pending ones, at 5 of 12 observed.

  1. Do the pending dots read as "not yet counted", or as a second series? They must not look like data of a different kind.
  2. Can you count what is left? "Seven still to come" should be readable without the caption, because that is what tells a reader the answer is still moving.
  3. At n = 60 the pending row is crowded. Whichever wins has to survive that, not just the default twelve.
