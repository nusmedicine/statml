§1 · The tasks, measured (_lab/pretrained-measure.mjs)

The source. Four kinds of widget 61's cell, each with a feature of its own — Nucleus · Granules · Ghost · Thick membrane — 200 images at 16 × 16, 20 epochs, the net 64 shipped (conv 8/12/16, 2,768 parameters): 0.8 s, 92–95 % on held-out images over three seeds. The first draft (nucleus · granules · plain · ghost) reached 59 % in the same budget and 84 % only at 800 images and 4.7 s; a source that is barely learned has no retention to lose gradually, so the classes were changed before the target was measured.

The target. A two-class distinction the source never labelled, fine-tuned from the source backbone with a fresh two-class head for a fixed 64 steps of batch 16 (an epoch budget makes the learning rate partly a “how much training” dial, the planning script's finding). Three candidates; round against elongated is the one that is learned. The far domain is the same cells with contrast inverted. What fixed steps cost. At 128 steps the source retention of 1e-4 has fallen to where 1e-3's has been since step 16 (near, n = 32: 67 / 48 against 86 / 56), and the ordering is a matter of when you read. At 64 steps it is clean in every cell, and the reader sees the reason on the strips: 1e-3 loses the source in the first sixteen steps, 1e-4 loses it a little every step.

One seed at a time. The widget shows one seed, so the per-seed check matters more than the means: at 64 steps, 1e-3 retains LESS of the source than 1e-4 in 22 of 24 seed-cells (near and far, four n, three seeds) and scores higher on the target in 20 of 24; the four target failures are cells where 1e-4 also learned the target (88 against 87), a tie rather than a reversal. The seed trained on this page is one of those: near, n = 32, both rates reach about 90 % on the new task and 1e-3 keeps 68 % of the source against 1e-4's 87 %. The Seed control is where the reader finds the seed that fails (2.6).
