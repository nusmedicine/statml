§2 · Breaking the assumptions — the generator, and what each plot detects

The design question the handover flags: the real data's plots are (mostly) healthy, so the diagnostics have nothing to detect until the reader can break an assumption. The generator keeps the real fit's own line (b₀ 87.07, b₁ 1.72, the real BMI values) and breaks exactly one thing at a time — seeded, so every URL reproduces. Settings below are the measured legibility thresholds. Each row: the data, then what Residuals-vs-Fitted sees, then what the Q-Q sees. Note the cross-talk, which is honest: strong curvature also bends the Q-Q (a wrong model class makes big standardized residuals), and the fan thickens the Q-Q tails. Frames are FIXED across all four rows (2.5) so a scenario switch reads as the data changing, not the axes.

BMI's density hides the funnel (most patients sit left, the wide right is sparse — judged on the row above), so the fan may need a MARK: rails at ±2 local SD. Flat rails = equal variance; a funnel = heteroskedasticity. On the real data the rails also carry the "horizontal band" bullet.

skewed noise — non-normal residuals (log-normal)

The control question: a segmented Data control naming the scenarios (Framingham · curved · unequal spread · skewed noise — each a named violation the reader diagnoses) versus two dials (curvature, fanning) that bend one synthetic dataset continuously. The segmented form matches the arc's vocabulary and keeps each violation clean; dials let a reader watch a violation GROW into the plots, at the cost of a two-parameter space where most corners break two assumptions at once. A middle path: segmented scenario + one strength slider that scales the chosen violation from 0 to the drawn setting.

§4 · Adjusted R² — where the bias argument actually fires

The notebook's claim: R² is biased upward as covariates are added; adjusted R² accounts for it. Measured: at n = 3547 the bias is invisible — ten pure-noise covariates move R² from 0.106 to 0.109 while adjusted stays 0.106. At n = 30 the same ten move R² from 0.15 to 0.45 while adjusted wanders around 0.11. So the lesson has two candidate homes: tiles only (R² and adjusted R² in the readout, always in agreement on this data — the bias stays a sentence), or a small-n act: a seeded subsample of 30 patients, a "junk covariates" stepper, and the two paths drawn live — R² climbing on noise alone, adjusted refusing.
