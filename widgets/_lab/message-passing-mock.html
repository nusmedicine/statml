4 · What the lesson says, and what the measurement says (findings 11–15, after the arc mock's ten)

  1. 09-2 cell 63's GAT formula sums over N(i) without the atom itself; GATConv adds a self-loop by default, and cell 64 keeps it, so each atom also attends to itself (the self-loop in your figure).
  2. Cell 64 passes edge_attr to both GAT layers; cell 42's GCN gets none. GCN against GAT therefore changes two things at once — learned weights and bond features — and the two models' F1 cannot say which helped.
  3. Cell 32's features enter the first layer unscaled: atomic number (6 to 83) beside 0/1 columns. Before any layer, caffeine's atoms have a mean cosine distance of 0.08 — every vector points the same way — against 0.94 with the columns standardised. BatchNorm comes after the first convolution, not before it.
  4. Cell 40 (global_max_pool, suitable for smaller molecules): under the lesson's untrained 2-layer GCN, max pooling gives identical vectors to 8–9 pairs of different molecules in the file — chain-length homologues, and aminoglycoside salts that differ only in the number of sulfate counter-ions. Mean and sum tie none. All tied pairs share a label, so no score in the lesson is affected.
  5. Cell 63 (attention scores provide a natural importance signal): trained three times on the same data, layer 2 puts ≥ 0.93 on one input for most atoms, but the three runs agree on that input for ? of caffeine's 14 atoms; layer 1 agrees on ?. The signal is sharp and not reproducible — 87's point, on this lesson's model.
