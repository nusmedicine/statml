1 · The press that opens the signatures

What the draft does now (A): a crossfade. The matrices are gone by the halfway point of 0.7 s and the signatures fade in from there, so nothing on screen says that each signature is a column of S and its exposures are a row of W. B and C move them. In both, S's column k lies down and stretches into signature k's place; its 96 cells then trade shade for height (the value moves from the shade to the bar's height, which frees colour to carry the class). W's row k slides under it, its 101 cells become bars in tumor order, and then they sort, largest first, which is the order the strip is read in. M fades as they leave. B moves every signature at once; C moves one signature at a time with the pair in flight outlined in the highlight colour, so column k and row k are seen to travel together. Tumor 101 (the bar switch): M's column for the hypermutated tumor is outlined in the draft; with this on, its cell in W keeps that outline through the move and the sort, so in the opened view its bar is marked in every strip — first in signature 4, near the end in the other three.

2 · The matrices' colour

The lesson's own M ≈ S · W figure (cell 0, the one page 2 is drawn from) shades its matrices cream → orange → red, and widget 41 draws its NMF matrices on the collection's value ramp, which ends in the same red. Red is C>T here, and C>T is the hottest block of M, so a red cell beside the red C>T band would say "many" and "C>T" in one colour. Grey is not free of that either: C>G is black and T>A is grey, so the draft's ramp ends between two of the class colours. Four ramps, each built only from colours the palette already has, and the numbers under them are measured from the tokens on this page in both themes.
