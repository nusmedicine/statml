1 · What the tools say, in their own words

Neither scrublet nor R is on this machine, so this is read from the packages' own documentation rather than run. The two leading tools disagree about the order, and the disagreement is the honest answer to your first question.

“Ensure that input data is cleared of low-quality cell clusters … Manually threshold raw gene expression matrices according to RNA nUMIs … Pre-process data using standard workflow … Identify clusters with (A) low RNA UMIs, (B) High % mitochondrial reads … Remove clusters, pre-process again, and run DoubletFinder.” DoubletFinder README, ‘Best-Practices’ — so: thresholds first, doublets after. Its input is “a fully-processed Seurat object (i.e., after NormalizeData, FindVariableGenes, ScaleData, RunPCA…)”.
“The input to scDblFinder should not include empty droplets, and it might be necessary to remove cells with a very low coverage (e.g. <200 or 500 reads) to avoid errors. Further quality filtering should be performed downstream of doublet detection, for two reasons: 1. the default expected doublet rate is calculated on the basis of the cells given, and if you excluded a lot of cells as low quality, scDblFinder might think that the doublet rate should be lower than it is. 2. kicking out all low quality cells first might hamper our ability to detect doublets that are formed by the combination of a good quality cell with a low-quality one. This being said, these are mostly theoretical grounds, and unless your QC filtering is very stringent (and it shouldn’t be!), it’s unlikely to make a big difference.” scDblFinder vignette, FAQ 1.5.11 — so: a floor first, the rest of the filtering after.

What they agree on, which is what the widget should carry: (1) drop the empty droplets before scoring — every version of the order starts there; (2) the score is computed in the expression space, after normalisation and PCA, not on the three raw numbers — it is not a fourth measurement of the same kind; (3) one sample at a time. DoubletFinder: “Do not apply DoubletFinder to aggregated scRNA-seq data representing multiple distinct samples … artificial doublets will be generated from WT and mutant cells, which cannot exist in your data”; scDblFinder takes a samples argument for the same reason. (4) Both are for heterotypic doublets only — scDblFinder: “has only a low performance in identifying homotypic doublets”; DoubletFinder: “insensitive to homotypic doublets”. Which is the 0 of 13 we measured.

And one thing measured here, because it decides what this widget can honestly show. Per-sample scoring is worse on this stage — but only because the stage draws 400 droplets a sample so they can be seen. At a real lane's size the two agree:

2 · Your question: is the score a fourth metric?

The strongest argument for putting it on Metrics is that it looks like one — a number per droplet, with a distribution. Here are the four side by side, computed on this stage, so you can see whether it sits comfortably:

What the picture does not say, and the page would have to: the first three are properties of a droplet and can be read off it alone. The fourth is a property of a droplet in a set — it needs every other droplet, a cloud of made-up doublets, and a decision about which droplets were in the set at all. Change the other three thresholds and every score moves.
