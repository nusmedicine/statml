The null, the tails, and what p actually counts

Shuffle the labels, recompute the difference, drop it into the pile. That pile is the null — the differences you would get if the labels meant nothing. p is the share of it at least as far from zero as the difference you actually saw.

The idea under test: a dot that lands in a tail stays lit. Arrivals are highlighted while falling anyway; one that lands beyond the line keeps that colour, one that lands in the middle settles to the ordinary fill. So p is “count the lit ones” rather than an abstract tail area — and the lit count is exactly the numerator in the tile below. Uses the real createPile and makePlot from core.

Two studies, same machinery, same shuffles. Left has a real effect; right has none at all — its true effect is exactly zero. Press New study a few times and watch the right-hand p bounce around: every one of those is a study where the null is true. Roughly one in twenty will still land under 0.05.

  1. Can you actually count the lit dots while the count is small? That is the claim. If they are not countable at 10–30 shuffles, p is back to being an area and the idea has failed.
  2. Do the two rules read as “this far out, either way”? Two-sided means the mirrored line matters as much as the observed one.
  3. Is the shaded tail doing any work the lit dots are not? If not it is furniture and should go.
  4. Does the right-hand cell make the false positive land? Press New study until it does. That is the whole case against “p is the probability the null is true”.
