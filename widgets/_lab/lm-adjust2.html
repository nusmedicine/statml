§1 · Covariate switches as pills

Two dressings of the same two booleans. Pills read as "what is IN the model" — membership, not settings — which is the right metaphor here. Both keep the checkbox's contract (independent toggles, four states, keyboard reachable); only the clothes change. This would be a small core addition (a pill style on bool), so the full suite runs when it lands — the rail is outside both hashes, but the rule is about core, not hashes.

§2 · The diagram — covariates as a picture, maybe as the control

05-02's own cell 9 draws this triangle to say why one-at-a-time fits mislead. Two treatments: A the diagram as a DISPLAY beside the forest — solid arrows for covariates in the model, faint for out, and the age–BMI link drawn as a dashed double-headed ASSOCIATION, labelled with its measured r = 0.12 (an arrow age → BMI would claim a causal direction this widget has no business asserting — that lesson lives in fork-pipe-collider, and the dashing keeps the two widgets honest with each other). B the same diagram as the CONTROL: covariate nodes are click targets that toggle membership — widget 26's clickable-node pattern — with the pills of §1 remaining as the keyboard path (5.6: a region is a shortcut, never the only route). A would replace nothing; B would replace nothing either — it adds regions and therefore a hit-driven fingerprint state.

§3 · "1D → 2D" — two readings of the ask, both drawn

Reading 1 — the model table: the notebook's own closer, three models side by side, the way modelsummary prints them. It could open the widget (the "initial table") or sit as a readback in the rail. Reading 2 — the geometry: one covariate fits a line; two fit a PLANE, and over BMI the plane is a family of parallel lines, one per age — ggPredict's own picture, drawn here at ages 35 / 45 / 55 / 65 (same slope 1.499, lifted 0.836 per year). The family is what "age held constant" LOOKS like, and it may do the 1D → 2D teaching better than a table of numbers.

§4 · The structure — gates, tabs, or a split (prose, for the call)

The three concepts are one notebook, one dataset, one machinery — which argues against a SPLIT into more widgets (the dimred split was four algorithms and four optimisers; this is one model read three ways). The current draft stages them by REVEAL: empty → covariates (fitting, adjusting) → twin gate (collinearity). The crowding Kenneth feels is real at the full act (forest + scatter + strip + VIF, 642px). The middle road is a concept strip — a segmented control (Fit the model · Adjust · Collinearity) that stages WHAT IS SHOWN while the model switches persist underneath: Fit shows scatter + line-family + equation (+ the table); Adjust shows the forest + ghosts + FWL slide; Collinearity shows the twin act. Each tab one idea at projector size; the machinery and the reader's model survive tab changes (display param, 3.4c's map for any per-tab labels). Cost: a real re-staging of draw(), and the fingerprint needs states per tab. The cheap alternative — keep gates, tighten signposting — is one more section heading and no new structure.
