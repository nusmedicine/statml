cell-qc · the three rows, drawn instead of described

The rows say “Two different types”, “Two of the same type”, “One cell”, and you have to hold all three in your head to read the figure. A mark can say it instead: one circle is one cell, two joined circles are two cells in one droplet, and the colours are which cell types they are. Four ways to do it, with the row as it would actually look under each.

1 · The glyph on its own, at the size it would be drawn

The middle pair is the hard one: two of the same type has to read as two cells and not as one fat cell, which is why the two circles are drawn apart rather than merged.

3 · And the same marks where the droplets are

If the glyph means something, the map can use it too: a droplet holding two cells drawn as two circles, each half the colour of the type it holds. Shown here on the cell map from the Thresholds page.

Against: the map already colours a droplet by what it HOLDS (blue one cell, red dying, grey empty, violet two cells), so giving the doublets a shape as well as a colour is one signal twice. For it: the shape is legible at 2px where a violet-versus-blue difference is not, and it ties the map to the neighbourhood panel above it.
