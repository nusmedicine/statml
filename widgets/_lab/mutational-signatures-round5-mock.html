1 · The page: Catalogue · Rank · Signatures · Matching

A new second page, in the notebook's order: cell 19 comes after the 96-type matrix and before extractSignatures. Each press runs one rank, 2 to 8 (the notebook's nTry in miniature): ten starts from ten random points, and after each start every pair of tumors that start put in one group (each tumor grouped by its largest signature) adds a tenth to its cell — so the matrix on the left builds start by start, and cells stay pale where the starts disagree. The tumors are ordered by the average-linkage tree of the finished matrix, so agreement shows as solid squares. Once the tenth start is in, the cophenetic correlation is printed under the matrix and that number slides to its place on the chart, which is cell 20's plot. After a press, clicking a point (or the Signatures to extract slider, which this page shares with Signatures and Matching) opens that rank's matrix; the rank chosen here is the one the Signatures page extracts. ▲ marks the hypermutated tumor, as on page 2.

2 · What the page is built to say

(a) What the number measures. Cell 19 describes the cophenetic correlation as reflecting "how closely clustering … represents the original mutation frequencies". It measures agreement between repeated runs: how consistently ten starts group the same tumors together (a 1 when every start agrees, falling as starts split pairs differently). It never looks at the counts. The matrix makes that visible: rank 2 is solid blocks, rank 8 is grey. (This was finding 6 in the list told to you on 2026-09-18.)
(b) The balance cell 19 names is two curves. The fit (the divergence left after the best start) only improves as rank goes up — it cannot choose a rank on its own. The agreement falls. The fit panel draws the first, the chart the second; the choice is where agreement is still high and the fit has stopped improving much. Off, the page is only the notebook's plot.
(c) The elbow is a judgement, not a rule. With True number on, the planted number of signatures is marked (4 processes; 5 with the hypermutated tumor in, since it carries its own). On seed 1 the largest drop is 4 → 5 and the notebook's reading ("the smallest rank where the coefficient drops significantly before it plateaus or increases") lands on 5, the truth. Other seeds do not always (§ 3).

4 · What it costs, and the consequence

One rank is ten runs to NMF's connectivity stop: about 7 s a rank in the browser's engine (1,339 iterations a run on average), 50 s for ranks 2–8. compute() cannot do that on a parameter change. So the page reads a table computed ahead by a script in _lab/, as widget 65 reads its trained networks: per seed, the ten starts' groupings at each rank with the tumor in and out (the matrix and the number are rebuilt from them in milliseconds). About 14 KB a seed. Seeds 1–20 is 280 KB; all 200 is 2.8 MB — which is the question in § 5. The table is regenerated whenever the cohort or the engine changes, and the verify checks a few of its cells against a live run.
Also: the rank slider goes to 6 today; this page needs 2–8 so the fall past the truth is visible, which makes the Signatures and Matching pages reach rank 8 as well (their heights already read the rank).
