Drawing 385 edges without a hairball

Kenneth’s diagram for widget 22 draws the graph of neighbourhood probabilities on the points, and he chose all edges with opacity proportional to μ over drawing only one sample’s. The concern was legibility: at the widget’s stage — 48 samples, four groups — n_neighbors = 15 gives 385 edges, and 40 gives 1040. This page is that decision at real panel size, so it is settled by looking rather than by arguing. Every μ here is computed live by widgets/umap/model.js, which is the module the widget itself runs — not a copy of it.

The four mappings differ only in how μ becomes ink. Linear is the literal reading of “opacity proportional to μ”. Gamma bends it so weak edges fade faster than they would linearly. Width-and-opacity spends the weight on both channels. Top-k-per-point draws each sample’s strongest edges only, which is the sparsest thing that is still every sample’s own neighbourhood. The panel size is 300 × 300, which is roughly what a 2×2 widget gives each quadrant at the 900px breakpoint.

What to look for

Whether the four groups are still four groups. The edges exist to show that the graph is what UMAP carries across, so if they fill the panel the points they connect stop being visible — and the points are the data. Move n_neighbors to 40 for the worst case and to 3 for the lesson’s own, where the graph is sparse but every edge is nearly the same weight.
