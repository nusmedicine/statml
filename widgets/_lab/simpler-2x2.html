A simpler 2×2 — what should widget 12 actually be?

Three complaints from the first build, and this page is the answer to all three. The view buttons were section headings pretending to be controls. “True risk ratio” was wrong — it says there is a population parameter and we are sampling from it, and nothing here samples anything. And the ratios were inputs, when a ratio is the one thing in this topic that should always be an output.

A · You set the four counts. Everything else is arithmetic.

Two arms of 100. Drag how many died in each. Nothing is drawn, nothing is sampled, there is no seed and no “true” anything — the risk ratio and the odds ratio are computed in front of you and are the only numbers on screen you did not set. One toggle: what the deaths are measured against.

  1. Is “out of everyone” / “per survivor” better than “risk” / “odds”? It names what the control does instead of naming the jargon, and the jargon then appears on the figure as the result of doing it.
  2. Set both sliders to the same number. Both ratios go to 1.00 together. Then raise them both — still 1.00, both of them. No effect is no effect on either scale, which is worth meeting early.
  3. Now set 90 and 45. The risk ratio is 2.00 and the odds ratio is 11.00. Same “twice the risk”, and one of the two numbers has left the building.

B · Cohort or case-control: which side of the table did you fill in first?

SUPERSEDED MODEL — do not read the numbers. This panel forms its case-control by thinning the survivors 1-in-k, which widget 12 no longer does: at k = 1 it is the cohort, so the control makes the distinction look like it is about sampling fraction when it is about which margin you fixed. The widget now enrols every case and r controls per case from a source population both studies sample. The framing this panel settled — which side did you fill in first, boxed = chosen — survived and is in the widget; the arithmetic did not. Panel A above is unaffected.

The same 200 patients, studied two ways. A cohort starts with the exposure and waits — you pick 100 infected and 100 not, and you count how many die. A case-control starts with the outcome and looks back — you pick the people who died and some who did not, and you count how many were infected. The numbers you picked are printed in a box; the numbers you counted are not.

  1. Read the death rate under each panel. The cohort says 30%, which is a fact about the disease. The case-control says whatever your slider says — and the slider is about your budget, not about anybody's illness. That is the whole argument, and it needs no algebra.
  2. Does the boxed / not-boxed distinction land? It is trying to say you chose this number, so you may not report it as a finding without using the word “margin”.
  3. Is the odds ratio line worth keeping here? It stays at 2.67 in both panels at every slider setting, which is the reason odds ratios exist — but it is also a fourth number on a page whose point is already made by the third.

The question this page exists to settle: one widget or two? A and B are different lessons. A is two ways to divide; B is which studies may divide which way. The first build made them two views of one widget and the view switch was the first thing to confuse a reader. The alternatives are: put B behind a gate at the bottom of A so it is a second stage rather than a second view, or make B its own widget and let A be small and sharp.
