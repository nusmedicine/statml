Shuffling the labels — two candidates

Widget 5 asks could chance alone have produced what I saw? Under the null the grouping is meaningless, so any relabelling of the same numbers is equally likely. The animation has one job: make it undeniable that the data never changes and only the labels move — which is also the exact opposite of the bootstrap, where the values move and the labels are irrelevant.

Both cells run the same seeded data and the same sequence of shuffles, in lockstep. The faint vertical guides mark each observation's value, so you can check whether anything has actually moved.

  1. Does A make “the values never move” obvious? That is the whole claim. If it does not land here it will not land in a lecture.
  2. Does B accidentally read as the data changing? The dots travel vertically. Watch whether your eye reports motion as “this observation moved” rather than “this observation was relabelled”.
  3. Which makes the two group means and the gap easier to read? The gap is the test statistic; it has to be legible every frame.
  4. Are the two group colours distinguishable at a glance, and from the back of a room? See the flag below.

Token gap this mockup exposes. There is no semantic role for “one arm of a comparison”. The five roles are empirical, theory, smoothed, highlight, reference, and none of them means “group B” — so this page borrows --series-1 and --series-2 directly, which the house rules forbid in a real widget. Widgets 4, 5 and 6 all show two groups, so this wants deciding once: probably --c-group-a / --c-group-b in tokens.css. Note --series-2 is also --c-theory; that collides if a figure ever shows two groups and a theoretical curve.
