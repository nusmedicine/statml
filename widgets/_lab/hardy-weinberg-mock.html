hardy-weinberg · the figure, the source, the sample size, the count

Slot 56 hardy-weinberg, PHM5003 week 6 QC. The one thing: one allele frequency predicts the three genotype frequencies (p², 2pq, q²), a sample that sits off that prediction has fewer heterozygotes when two populations are pooled or when heterozygotes are miscalled, and the χ² test sees only a deviation the sample size allows. One page, one figure.

The measurement. Every number and every point on this page is computed live by the code copied verbatim out of _lab/hwe-measure.mjs — erfc, chi1Tail, hweTest, chi1Crit, poolSample, dropoutSample, overcallSample, wahlundF, comments and all — drawn with the seeded makeRng from widgets/core/rng.js. Nothing is drawn from a remembered number, and the page cannot drift from the survey that chose these values. One function is added and marked: poolStream, which is poolSample's own loop keeping the running table after each individual, for §5.

§0 · The title and the subtitle

Both candidates rendered in the shell's own .w-heading at the stage's 550px, with the character count measured against the 140–240 the recent subtitles hold (2.10).

§1 · The figure

All three drawn from one sample: two populations pooled, allele frequency 0.5, frequency difference 0.5, 323 individuals, seed 1 — the deficit the test just sees at the lesson's own sample size. The de Finetti triangle's one property that makes it worth the space: a genotype table's horizontal position is its allele frequency (x = fAa/2 + faa = q exactly), so the observed point and its prediction share a vertical, and the heterozygote deficit is the length of that drop.

§2 · What produced the sample

Four sources at 323 individuals, allele frequency 0.5, seed 4 — the one-population panel at seed 21, the nearest seed at which its sample lands on the curve rather than on the excess seed 4 happens to draw there; both readings are measured under the figures. The pooled panel draws the two populations' own points on the curve and the line between them: the pooled sample is the average of the two tables, so it sits on that line, and the line lies under the curve. That is the whole Wahlund argument in one picture, and it is the reason the deficit has nothing to do with genotyping.

§3 · Sample size

Two populations pooled with a frequency difference of 0.17 — the average real SNP's deficit (F 0.03), not the extreme one §1 draws. Five sample sizes, the same seed. Row A is the whole triangle, drawn at each size. Row B is the same five as a detail of the triangle around the point, one fixed window for all five, with 24 further draws behind the seeded one so the sampling scatter is visible as scatter.

§4 · The count

A core's own readout tiles under the figure. B a table drawn on canvas in the layout of PLINK's .hwe file. Both show the same two samples: the pooled one from §1 and one population at the same allele frequency and size.

And the threshold. Fixed at 10−6, or a three-rung choice. The table is the same sample read against each of the three.

§5 · The arrival

A Draw lands the finished sample at once. B individuals arrive one at a time: the arriving one is a tick on its own bar, the bars grow, and the observed point walks from its first position to where it settles. B is drawn as three stills of one run — the same 323 individuals as §1, after 10, 60 and 323 of them.

§6 · Many SNPs

A one SNP, the widget as described above. B a second page: 2,000 SNPs drawn at the chosen source and sample size, a histogram of the heterozygote deficit F, the threshold's F line, and the count past it. Drawn here at two populations pooled, 323 individuals, each SNP's mean allele frequency uniform on 0.1–0.9 and its frequency difference a half-normal chosen so the mean F matches the lesson file's 0.030.
