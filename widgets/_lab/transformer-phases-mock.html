Your question: in practice, is everything in parallel? should we split into training and inference? The notebooks draw the same line: attention processes all positions in parallel, which is what makes pretraining feasible (08-1 cells 0 and 4, 05-3 cell 20, 08-3 cell 1); and "during training, the model sees the full sequence … during generation, it must rely entirely on its own past predictions, which makes generation a sequential process" (10-3 cell 45). Teacher forcing and the key-value cache are named nowhere. Every number below is from the draft's own models, _lab/transformer-phases-measure.mjs.

T1 decoder, one pass over the whole sentence against the sentence built a token at a time: every row's weights and every next-token distribution identical (largest difference 0.0). The mask is what lets training run in one pass. T2 encoder: training (masked tokens) and inference (the tokens' vectors) are both ONE pass over the whole sentence. aspirin masked: aspirin 0.61, nitrate 0.39 from the one pass. T3 encoder-decoder, the whole protein at once (training, the true protein given) against a prefix at a time (generation): identical self- and cross-attention rows (0.0). T4 rows computed for 7 tokens: one training pass 7, at once; generation keeping the earlier rows 7, over 7 steps; generation recomputing everything 28. So: the ENCODER always runs in parallel; the DECODER runs in parallel in TRAINING and one token at a time in GENERATION. Within a row, a real computation is one matrix product: the cell-by-cell sweep is a way of showing, not the order of the arithmetic.
