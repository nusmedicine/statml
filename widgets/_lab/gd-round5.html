Widget 48 · round 5 — the gradient's direction, the tangent's motion, and a Gradients widget

§1 · "Why doesn't the gradient go downhill directly?" — it does, and this is what actually happens

The gradient is the direction of steepest ascent in parameter units, perpendicular to the contour through the point; a step of gradient descent follows the steepest slope, not the shortest line to the minimum. On the lesson's raw-x surface the loss is 138× more curved along b₁ than along the trench, so the steepest slope at almost every point is across the trench: the walk drops to the floor in a few epochs and then creeps along it. At the state in Kenneth's screenshot (lr 0.003, epoch 10) the step points 74° above the b₀ axis and the straight line to the minimum points 5° below it — 80° apart. It is not coordinate descent (widget 27's walk, one parameter at a time); it only looks like it because the map magnifies b₁ 2.5× relative to b₀. Standardizing x makes both curvatures 2, and then the gradient does point at the minimum. The widget draws all of this and says none of it; that is the defect. The three panels are the same walk, each with the gradient's direction (purple), the straight line to the minimum (dashed red) and the contour's tangent at the point (grey).

What the widget should gain from this: (a) a line under the surface stating the angle — "the step follows the steepest slope, 80° from the straight line to the minimum" — computed from the state, so it reads 3° on the standardized bowl; (b) the straight line to the minimum drawn faint so the reader can see the two directions disagree; (c) possibly the equal-aspect map (B), where "perpendicular to the contour" is true on screen. The relief already shows the steep wall and the flat floor.

§2 · The one-parameter tangent — five ways to move it (live)

Kenneth: the tangent "moves then redraws (like expanding out) at each step" — the current choreography grows the segment from its centre at every epoch after having rolled with the curve. Each panel loops eight epochs of the lesson's walk at lr 0.01 from b₁ = 0. E is the current behaviour. A the tangent is always there at full length and rolls with the point; each epoch pauses at the point (the arrow and its number), then moves with an ease. B the tangent fades out as the point leaves and fades in when it lands; nothing grows. C the tangent stays put at the start while the point departs along it and slides down to the curve; at arrival a fresh tangent fades in. D no pause at all: the point rolls continuously with an ease, the tangent and the arrow roll with it, only the number updates at arrival.

§3 · "Consider basics first" — a Gradients widget with tabs

The lesson (Tensors and Gradients) reaches optimization through three ideas in order: a derivative is how much y changes for a small change in x; a partial derivative varies one variable with the others held, and the gradient is the vector of them (y = a² + 3ab at a = 2, b = 1 → 7 and 6, which autograd prints); then gradient descent uses that vector. Proposed: rename widget 48 to Gradients, three tabs — Derivative · Partial derivatives · Descent — where Descent is the current widget (its one/two-parameter switch and the relief intact). The two new tabs are mocked below on the lesson's own function, so the numbers are the lesson's numbers. Tensors and the linear algebra are a separate question (a shape-and-matmul widget is slot 49's territory); they do not belong in a widget about gradients.
