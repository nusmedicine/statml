Widget 31 · time-event — the stage, drawn before it is argued

The misconception (catalogue slot 5): censored patients are missing data to discard. Measured first (node widgets/_lab/time-event-measure.mjs, 30 checks — the five-patient KM to the digit against cell 8's stored plot; the Cox engine against an independent naive likelihood and the tie-free log-rank≡score identity, since cell 16 stored no output). The decisive finding: the notebook's own simulated arm cannot host the lesson — its Status is a fair coin drawn independently of time, so a censored patient is censored AT the very time the event would have happened; under that design KM lies HIGH (+0.13) and discarding is the unbiased choice. With honest study-end censoring (observed = min(T, C), doors close at 20) the roles are what the lesson says: KM −0.0002 from truth, discarding −0.081, curve LIES LOW. §4 · The hazard function, drawn — research and four candidates

What the literature offers (researched 2026-08-29): the established teaching visual is Singer & Willett's PAIRED PROFILE — hazard per interval beside the survival curve, read as "when hazard is high the survivor drops fast". The analytic route (h = −slope of log S, cumulative hazard) has no good geometry and is beyond the course. No explorable explanation of hazard exists to borrow from — the interactive tools out there are analytics dashboards. The widget-native translation: the discrete hazard d/n is already on screen — its denominator is tab 1's at-risk count, and censoring's whole role (cell 2's bullet: censored patients count in P(T ≥ t) until they leave) is a change to that denominator. H1 pairs a hazard strip under the five-patient curve: bars 1/5, 1/4, 1/2 — nothing at censor times, and the t = 8 halving IS the tallest bar. H2 keeps one panel and annotates each step's relative drop on the curve itself. H3 is the groups-tab version: life-table bins, h = d/n at bin start — disease above no-disease in every bin on seed 1, ordering held on 100/100/100/98/78 of 100 seeds per bin (the last bin has 2 at risk — the small-denominator caveat, itself teachable). H4 is the Cox card's own hazard picture: per-bin rates (events per person-time — a rate, as cell 2 defines h), with the one-covariate Cox claim drawn over them: every baseline bar × the SAME exp(β). Proportional means the whole strip scales.
