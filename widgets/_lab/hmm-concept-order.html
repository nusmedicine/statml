Widget 45 · Concept tab — inverted to follow the lesson: tosses as states first, the coin as the hidden state second

What the tab does now: the chain is over the coins, Fair and Loaded, shown first with the coin visible and then with it hidden. What the lesson now says: a simple Markov model is a coin toss whose states are Heads and Tails; a hidden Markov model then puts the coin, Fair or Loaded, behind the tosses. The mocks below invert the tab to that order. Everything is drawn static; the walk, the hover and the choreography carry over unchanged, only what the nodes are changes. One sticky-coin record (stay 0.80) is used for the Markov stage and one casino record (stay 0.80, loaded coin Heads 0.80) for the hidden stage.

A · Two stages, the lesson's order — a segmented control, Markov model | Hidden Markov model, replacing the coin's Visible | Hidden

Stage 1's counts table is an estimate of T: the states are in the record, so counting transitions is how the table is learned. Stage 2 keeps the same counts table over the same kind of record, and now it is not T, because the record holds the tosses and the states are the coins. That contrast is what the inversion buys: the student has just seen counting work, and then sees exactly why it stops working. The Show the ground truth toggle reveals the coin row in stage 2, which is what the current Visible mode showed, so nothing the tab does today is lost.

B · Three stages — the current visible-coins step kept as a middle stage: Tosses | Coins, seen | Coins, hidden

The middle stage shows the coins' own chain in the open before hiding it, which is what the tab does today. Cost: a three-way control whose middle option the lesson does not have, and a coin row that stage 3's ground-truth toggle already provides. I would not build this unless the middle step earns its place in the lesson text.

Questions to settle before building

  • The Markov coin has to be sticky. A fair coin with independent tosses has T rows of 0.50 / 0.50, which is a Markov chain with no memory, so nothing in stage 1 would move. The mock gives the toss a stay probability, P(next toss repeats the last), on the same slider the hidden stage uses for the coin. At 0.50 the slider reads as "independent tosses", which is itself a teaching point, but the lesson's coin-toss example should say the coin is sticky, or the widget will contradict it.
  • Two records, not one. Stage 1's tosses come from the sticky chain; stage 2's from the casino. They cannot be the same sequence, because the mechanism is the point. The rail's Seed covers both.
  • One slider or two. "P(stay)" means "repeat the last toss" in stage 1 and "keep the same coin" in stage 2. One control whose label changes with the stage, or two controls each shown in its stage. I would use two, gated by the stage, so a URL says which quantity it set.
  • The middle stage's counts. In A the coin-transition counts the current Visible mode shows go away; in stage 2 with ground truth on they could return as a second counts table beside the toss counts. Worth it only if you want the student to see the two tables side by side.
