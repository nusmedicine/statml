Which data the SVM widget stands on

The claim the widget would make: the boundary is set by the samples in the margin, and every other sample enters the objective at exactly zero. C is not an accuracy dial — it sets how wide the corridor is, and the corridor decides who counts.

What is open is which data that claim stands on, because it does not fire on both.

04-3’s own SVC example is the heart-failure cohort, and on it the story does not fire.
Two features carry signal — serum creatinine (AUC 0.728) and ejection fraction (0.676 inverted).
On that plane 179 of 299 patients are support vectors at C = 1, and never fewer than 175 anywhere on the ladder. There is no corridor; the corridor is the cohort.
Accuracy moves 0.689 → 0.732 across four orders of magnitude of C, and the count inside the margin moves by two patients. The chain does not move.
And the two features take only 17 and 40 distinct values, so 299 patients occupy 142 positions — and 21 of those hold both outcomes, 95 patients sitting exactly where someone with the opposite outcome sits. No line separates them, and no ring marks one of them.

03-5’s colorectal biopsies do fire.
194 samples, 97 normal and 97 adenocarcinoma; two probes, FXYD5 (dysadherin, up in tumour) against C7 (complement C7, down).
C sweeps the support vectors from 174 down to 3 while accuracy never leaves 1.000.
Tick fit on the support vectors only at the tight end: 191 samples leave and the boundary does not move — to nine decimal places, measured in the table at the foot.
Cost: it is 03-5’s data, not 04-3’s, and its axes are probe sets rather than a bedside measurement.

So: which stage — or does the widget carry both, as one control? Carrying both is the argument for principle 2.6: the biopsies say what a margin is, and the cohort is what the same method does when there is no gap. Both are drawn below at the same C.

S2 · the plane, with the loss beside it — a second, smaller question

The second panel is the hinge loss against the signed margin y f(x), with every sample’s own position marked underneath it. It answers why only those samples: past y f = 1 the loss is not small, it is exactly zero, so those samples enter the objective at nothing. The dashed curve is the logistic loss widget 15 minimises, which never reaches zero — there, every patient pulls.

Two things noticed while drawing it, neither decided

The boundary is on --c-highlight violet, not --c-theory. Orange and the red of --c-event are eleven degrees of hue apart, which HANDOVER already records as reading like one colour at a thin stroke. Support vectors are then ringed in --ink-1 rather than recoloured, so a support vector keeps saying which class it is. If this holds it wants a named role — five of the six PHM5005 algorithm widgets draw a decision boundary.

The loss panel rescales its own axes with C, so it jumps as you drag — that is 2.5 and it would be fixed before shipping, not left.
