Sorting the Replicate band's columns

DECLINED, 2026-09-05. A ships — unsorted, dots dissolve. Kenneth: "just do A ... sorting will introduce more need for explanation". The measurements below are real and the tween works, but every one of sorting's gains has to be explained before it is a gain: the axis stops meaning "person 1, 2, 3", the rising line has to be said not to be a trend, and the slope has to be named as the between-person spread before anyone reads it as one. That is three sentences of scaffolding on a tab that already carries two dials, two bands and a pile. Do not re-propose without a reason that is not on this page.

The dots dissolve between studies rather than moving, and the reason is that column p of one study and column p of the next are two unrelated people — sliding between them would say a person's value changed. Sorting each arm by the person's own mean removes that objection: column p becomes the p-th smallest person, an order statistic, which really is the same thing in both studies.

It also makes the move short. Measured over 200 consecutive study pairs, how far a column has to travel:
