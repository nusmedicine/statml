Kenneth (2026-09-08): the tangent lines on the Derivative tab are hard to see; try red. The tangent is drawn in --c-highlight, violet, and it lies ON the curve, which is --ink-2, so the contrast that matters is against that grey, not the background: violet against the curve is 1.08 in the light theme — the same luminance — and 1.74 in the dark. Red (--series-8) reaches 2.0 and 1.8, and it already means past a threshold and the hot end of the loss ramp, which the gradient arrow crosses on the Descent map. The table below is measured; every panel draws the Derivative figure (curve, secant, tangent, point) in a candidate, with the Descent map's ramp under it and the arrow across the ramp so the collision, or its absence, is visible. Contrast, measured (WCAG luminance ratio)

"vs curve" is the number that decides legibility of a line lying on the curve; "vs ramp high" is the arrow on the Descent map's hottest end, "vs ramp low" its floor. A casing halo makes any hue separable from the curve regardless of these numbers; it does not help against the ramp.

What the palette allows. --series-5 magenta is the one slot that carries no semantic role today (1 empirical/group-a/cost-low/value-low, 2 theory, 3 smoothed, 4 group-b/prior, 6 cluster-e, 7 highlight/posterior, 8 extreme/event/holdout/cost-high/value-high). A new role — the local slope: a tangent, a secant, a gradient arrow — on that slot is what CLAUDE.md prescribes for a role that does not exist ("a signal to add one, not to reach for --series-n"). It is a tokens.css change, so it owes a full fingerprint run, which is rendering-neutral for every widget that does not use it.
