Both draw the same claim, in your diagram’s words: PC1 is the direction along which the spread of the projected points is as large as possible. They differ in where the search happens, and that decides whether the reader ever sees the whole search or only a slice of it.

Widths are the real ones. The narrow canvas is 550 px, which is what the fingerprint harness renders at and roughly what a projector gives you; the wide one is 900 px. Everything is drawn from tokens.css — no hardcoded colour, size or font.

A — your diagram, animated: the plane turns through the 3-D cloud

Three axis arrows, nine samples in three groups, and a plane that turns. The readout is the variance of the points once projected onto the plane, and the small dial beside it is that same variance for every orientation in the family being swept — so “as large as possible” is something the reader watches happen rather than something a caption asserts. The plane stops at the maximum, the samples drop onto it, and it rotates flat to face you. That last rotation is the step your third panel is: the 2-D plot is the plane, seen face-on.

B — the search shown whole: one dimension lower, then lifted

The same mechanism with the search complete rather than sampled. In 2-D, a direction is one angle, so the variance-against-angle curve underneath is the entire search — every direction there is, with PC1 at the peak and PC2 ninety degrees away at the trough. Nothing is off-screen and nothing is a family chosen to contain the answer. The cost: it is not your diagram, and the reader meets 3-D only afterwards.

The honesty problem both share, and what it costs to fix

PCA does not search. It solves an eigenproblem; nothing rotates and nothing is tried. A turning plane is a didactic fiction — a good one, because it is the notebook’s own wording, but the widget must not end up claiming the method works this way. A is the more exposed of the two, because a plane sweeping one family of orientations invites “so it tries them all”, and it does not: the family is chosen to contain the answer. B is safer, because in 2-D the family really is every direction.

The cheap fix in either is one line under the dial naming what is plotted — the variance for each direction — and letting the reader see the peak, rather than a line saying the method looks for it.
