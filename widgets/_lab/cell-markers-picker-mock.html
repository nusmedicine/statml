cell-markers · Two clusters: choosing the comparator and the baseline

The draft has three ways to set the same two values (the rail's dropdowns, the rail's "A click sets" switch, and the chips beside the map), and a map whose clusters are small grey blobs with numbers. Below, three ways with one control per value, on the widget's own cells (seed 1, resolution 0.3). In each, a cluster is drawn as a shaded region around its cells, named by its cell type, and the region is what a click lands on. Every one of them is clickable here. The rail stays in each — the collection keeps a rail control for anything the canvas sets, as the keyboard and screen-reader route (principle: "keep the control") — but as one control per value, named by type.

A · Two maps: one for the comparator, one for the baseline

Each map sets one thing, so no switch says what a click does. The baseline map has "All other cells" as a target under it. The rail shrinks to the same two choices as a list of type-named buttons (or could collapse under a disclosure).

B · One map for the comparator, a row of buttons for the baseline

Clicking the map sets the comparator. The baseline is a row under the map: "All other cells" and each cluster by name. One large map; the second choice is the smaller one, so it takes the smaller control.

C · Buttons in the rail; the map shows the choice

Two rows of type-named buttons in the rail, and the map only shows what they chose (comparator filled, baseline outlined). The simplest to use and to read; nothing on the canvas is clicked (clicking still works as a shortcut, here, for comparison).
