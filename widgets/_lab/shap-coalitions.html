Widget 38 · seven ways to draw the same eight coalitions

Kenneth, 2026-08-31: “is there another way to depict the coalitions?” — yes, six more. Every panel below is the same eight numbers, for the same patient, under the same model: patient #50 (age 85, ejection fraction 60%, serum creatinine 3.2 mg/dL, survived), decision tree, predicted risk of death. The eight v(S) are brute-forced in _lab/shap-ref.py and re-derived by this page's own JS. Nothing here needs extra compute: pick on what a reader can see, not on cost.

§A · The path plot — v(S) on a real axis, |S| across

The one thing the lattice throws away is the value: its nodes are boxes in arbitrary positions. Here the vertical axis is the prediction, the horizontal axis is how many features have been revealed, and the eight coalitions sit where their prediction puts them. Every ordering is a path from the single left node to the single right node — so efficiency is the shape of the figure, and the spread between the highest and lowest path at rank 1 and 2 is the disagreement, at a glance.

§B · The cube — one axis per feature

With three features the lattice is a cube, and its three directions are the three features. Age's four marginal contributions are the four edges parallel to the age axis; their weights (⅓, ⅙, ⅙, ⅓) are how many of the six corner-to-corner routes use each. It is the most memorable object on this page and the least extensible: four features has no picture.

§C · The ordering tree — where the factorials come from

One root, three ways to pick who joins first, two ways after that: 1 → 3 → 6. An edge near the root is shared by two of the six orderings; an edge near a leaf by one. That is w(S) = |S|!(n−|S|−1)!/n! — count the leaves below an edge and divide by six. No other depiction on this page derives the weight rather than asserting it.

§D · The weighted average, drawn — one feature at a time

Four distinct marginal contributions, four weights. Bar length is Δ, row height is the weight, and φ is the line that balances them. This is the formula and nothing else — it does not show the coalitions at all, so it is a companion panel, not a stage.

§E · The 6 × 3 matrix — and the column you average

Six orderings down, three features across, every cell a Δ. The Shapley value is the bottom row. Compact enough to sit in a readout beside a bigger figure, and the only depiction where all eighteen marginal contributions are legible at once.

§F · Orderings arriving one at a time — what the library actually does

Eleven features have 39,916,800 orderings, so shap samples them and averages. With three you can exhaust the list, and the running mean walking onto the exact answer is the honest picture of an estimate. This is the one candidate that gives Play something true to do — every other panel here is finished the moment it is drawn.
