deseq2 · the Transform page: what the transform does for the plots

His question (round 8, 2026-09-22): is there a practical way of showing the importance of the transforms for visualisation? The notebook takes vsd for two figures, the PCA of the samples and the sample-correlation heatmap. Measured on the widget's own stage (1,200 genes, a tenth changed, 3 vs 3): under raw counts the top 1% of genes hold 67–90% of the total variance and PC1 does not split the two groups (between-group distance over within-group spread 1.2–2.2); under log2(x + 1) it does (2.8–4.2); under the vst it does best (3.5–5.5). Every panel below is computed here, three units across each row, for one seed. Blue and orange are the two groups (--c-group-a / --c-group-b).

A · the PCA of the samples, the notebook's own QC figure

One dot per sample on PC1 and PC2, the group as colour. Under counts the two highest genes decide where a sample lands; under the vst the groups fall apart along PC1. The tile would read the between/within ratio and the share of variance on PC1.

B · the sample-to-sample distance heatmap, the notebook's other QC figure

Euclidean distance between every pair of samples over all genes, bright is near. Under counts the pattern is not the groups; under the vst the two blocks appear.

C · which genes carry the variance

The cumulative share of the total variance against the genes ranked by their variance. Under counts a handful of genes are almost all of it, so the PCA and the heatmap are those genes; under the vst the curve is spread, so every gene has a say.

The call

Recommended: A as the Transform page's right panel in place of the transform-as-a-curve panel, since it is the figure the notebook draws next with vsd, and a control the reader already has (the unit) rewrites it. The SD-against-mean panel stays on the left as the cause; the tiles read the between/within ratio and the top 1% share. C is the sharpest single number and can live in a tile.
