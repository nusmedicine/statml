The follow-up ladder, remeasured (100 seeds per cell, n = 200, moderate effect, the shipping generator): today's 12/15/20/25 has no corner where a too-short study fails — detection is total from 12 up. The proposal is 5 · 9 · 12 · 25, four options, four lessons:

F=5  ·  3 events — log-rank fires 26%, Cox finds NOTHING (0% disease, 0% age, 0% SNPs; 88% of fits refuse with the too-few-patients line). The study was not run long enough — on BOTH cohort tabs.
F=9  ·  33 events — log-rank 99%, disease and age 100%, but each SNP only ~55% and all three together 15%: the strong factors survive a short study, the weak ones vanish.
F=12 · 83 events — the default: SNPs ~88% each, all three found on 70% of draws.
F=25 · 200 events of 200 — censoring dissolves entirely; everything found.

The causal-SNPs lever, measured (same cells): the generator parameterised so the first k of the ten SNPs truly shorten survival — k = 3 reproduces today's generator bit for bit (verified on seed 32), so the clean default draw survives. Proposal: Causal SNPs 0 · 1 · 3 · 5, default 3, in the data section beside Disease effect. Detection at F = 12: a lone causal SNP is found 76% of the time; each of three ~88%; each of five ~93%. One finding that needs a call: at k = 0 the twelve-covariate fit still stars at least one null SNP on 67% of draws (54 events across 12 covariates — the events-per-variable problem). The honest move is a computed note when k = 0: "no SNP truly shortens survival — a starred SNP here is a false positive", the same licence as Disease effect None's "no real difference".
