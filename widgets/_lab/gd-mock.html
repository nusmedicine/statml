Widget 48 · gradients — the surface, the step, the learning rate

The lesson's own example (05-2 cells 73–78): x = linspace(0, 10, 100), y = 5 + 2x + N(0, 1), the mean squared error, plain gradient descent from (0, 0) at lr 0.01 for 1000 epochs. Everything below is drawn on one seeded draw of that data. The measured fact every panel answers to (dl-gd-measure.mjs): the surface's two curvatures are 68.5 and 0.50, so the walk is stable only below lr 0.029, reaches the trench in three epochs and then crawls along it — the intercept's direction is 138× flatter than the slope's. Standardizing x makes both curvatures 2.

The surface ramp is log10 of loss ÷ least loss, capped at 316×: the start (0, 0) sits at ~300× and the trench is entered at ~8×, so widget 27's 3× cap would paint the whole walk solid. Contour lines are drawn at fixed ratios so the gradient's direction can be read against them.

§1 · Stage composition — the Two-parameters page

A the data and the current line beside the surface — widget 27's row, on this data, with the gradient at the current point and the path so far; B the surface with its two axis slices beside it, so the partial derivatives are the two tangent slopes the lesson defines them as; C A with a loss-per-epoch strip beneath (widget 37's strip), which is where the crawl is legible once the surface point has stopped moving visibly.

§2 · How one step is drawn

The honest problem: at the lesson's lr the first steps are huge (b₁ moves 0 → 1.84 in one epoch) and the trench steps are under a pixel (b₀ moves ~0.02 per epoch). A the step −lr·∇L to scale — true, and invisible in the trench; B the gradient's direction at a fixed length, perpendicular to the contour, its size only in the readout; C the two partials as component ticks along the axes, composing into the direction, each with its number. Same state as §1.

§3 · The learning rate, four values on the raw surface

The path after 300 epochs. The candidate lr choice must contain a divergent value — the claim is that above 2/curvature the walk blows up, and a control that cannot reach it cannot lose. The divergent path leaves the frame; the frame stays fixed (2.5) and the caption says at which epoch.

§4 · Standardizing x — the same walk on a round bowl

Standardized x: both curvatures are 2, so any lr below 1 is stable and lr 0.5 lands on the minimum in one step (the step is exactly 1/curvature). At the lesson's 0.01 it is still slow — 0.98 of the distance kept per step in both directions. So the picture says: standardizing does not make a walk fast, it lets the learning rate be large. Whether scale is a control here or the standardized bowl is the only stage is the pick.

§5 · The One-parameter page — the parabola

b₀ held at its fitted value, the loss as a curve over b₁, curvature 67.3: the three regimes on one parabola — converge below 1/curvature, oscillate between 1/ and 2/curvature, diverge above. Eight steps from b₁ = 0, the tangent drawn at the start. The pick: a page of its own before the surface, a strip under it, or cut because §1 B's slices already carry it.

§6 · Batches — the S in SGD

Raw x, lr 0.01, 20 epochs, the position after every update: full batch (one update per epoch), batches of 10 (ten per epoch), one row at a time (a hundred per epoch). Whether the gradient becomes an estimate is what a batch control would show; the numbers underneath say what it costs.
