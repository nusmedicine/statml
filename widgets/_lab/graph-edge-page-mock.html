94's edge-level example · mock

His ask: "we have an example for node prediction, can you suggest something for edge prediction? mock it." 09-1 names three task levels: a label for each node, for each edge, for the whole graph. Patients is the node level and Molecule the graph level, so a third example makes the set. What is new in the tables for an edge task: the label sits on a pair of nodes, so PyG adds edge_label_index (the pairs to score) and edge_label (1 for an edge, 0 for none). The pairs labelled 1 are known edges taken out of edge_index, since a model that passes messages along an edge has been shown its answer; the pairs labelled 0 are sampled from node pairs with no edge (RandomLinkSplit does both). A · Protein interactions: the split, in three steps

Ten DNA-damage-response proteins and twelve interactions well established in the literature (p53 with MDM2, p21, ATM, CHEK2; the BRCA1–BARD1, BRCA1–PALB2–BRCA2 and RAD51 complexes). A small illustrative piece, not a curated download. One rail control, Split All · Held out · Negatives: All — every known interaction in edge_index; Held out — two interactions (ATM–CHEK2, PALB2–BRCA2) leave edge_index and become the label-1 pairs; Negatives — two pairs that are not among the twelve (MDM2–RAD51, p21–BARD1) join as label 0, fixed here where PyG samples them. A negative is only a pair with no recorded edge: some sampled negatives are real interactions not yet found, the very thing the task looks for. Interactions here in STRING's sense, physical or functional (p53 and p21 are a transcription factor and its target).

The mistake the split prevents

If the held-out interactions stay in edge_index, the model scores ATM–CHEK2 after passing messages along ATM–CHEK2. This is the edge-level form of 97's leak (a test molecule's scaffold in training). Drawn here for the argument only; the recommendation is a note in the widget, not a fourth setting.

B · Drug–target: a bipartite graph

Five kinase inhibitors and five kinases, nine known drug–target pairs (imatinib: ABL1, KIT; dasatinib: ABL1, SRC, KIT; gefitinib, erlotinib: EGFR; lapatinib: EGFR, ERBB2). Held out: dasatinib–KIT (label 1). Negative: gefitinib–KIT (label 0). The rows of edge_index number drugs (0–4) and proteins (0–4) separately.

Recommended: A, Protein interactions, as a page named Interactions between Patients and Molecule, so the examples run node · edge · graph. It keeps one node type, so the only new tables are the two the edge task brings (edge_label_index, edge_label), and its nodes are proteins, as on the Graph page. B is the stronger drug-discovery story, but it adds a second idea at once (two node types, HeteroData), and C is too small. Each example page would then name its task level in a tile: y · [12] on Patients, edge_label · [4] here, y · [1] on Molecule. Still no training (that is 95).
