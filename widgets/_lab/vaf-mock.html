0 · The subtitle

Three claims that read written, in the register of the shipped ones. Nothing here describes the widget.

1 · The first page's stage: what a sample IS

The same sample three ways — purity 0.70, the mutation in every tumour cell, a diploid region, which cell 17 reads as VAF 0.35. Your figure draws a handful of large cells; a sample at 70% purity needs enough of them to be a proportion.

2 · The reads

The same 88 reads (the lesson's median depth) drawn two ways, with the expected VAF marked. A read is one draw from the sample's alleles, so reads arrive one at a time under Step and in a batch under Draw.

3 · One reading, three arrangements — the page's argument

All three read VAF 0.250: half the sample is normal cells; half the tumour cells carry it; it sits on one of four copies in every tumour cell. Nineteen arrangements in the swept grid read 0.25 ± 0.004. One equation, three unknowns — and no depth separates them.

4 · The second page: many mutations at once

300 mutations of one tumour at purity 0.70 and the lesson's depths: 180 in every tumour cell, 120 in half of them. The clusters are what a Gaussian mixture picked by BIC returns — mclust's rule, which cell 22 calls. Measured: at 300 mutations it gives a tumour with ONE clone two clusters in 21 of 30 runs.

5 · From VAF to cancer cell fraction

The same 300 mutations divided by cell 25's denominator. At the true purity the clonal peak lands on 1; taken as pure, it lands at 0.59 and the page's own cut empties.

6 · The third page: which trees the fractions allow

Your RETCHER figure's four samples, its three clusters' mean CCF. Two shapes are possible with three clusters; the rule is that no parent's children may sum past it. Measured: the first three samples rule out branching, the surgery sample alone allows both, and the four together leave the linear tree only.
