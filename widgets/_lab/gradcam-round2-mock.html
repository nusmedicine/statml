§1 · What the measurement says before any picture

The size of the mark and the size of the image are one decision, because the shortcut has to be EASIER than the cell to win. Cell against ghost is an easy task (100 % at 20 epochs), so only a blunt cue beats it. Three seeds, the small net, the mark on every training cell and no ghost:

So a small marker cannot be the shortcut on this task, a larger image makes every shortcut lose, and a subtler cue (a batch effect in the field's brightness, the other classic case) does not win either. What a small marker CAN do is sit on every image and be ignored: with a 3 × 3 marker on every training image the heat stays on the cell (62 % of it at 16, 67 % at 24, on a 30 % footprint), which is the picture your second point asks for.

§4 · The literature behind the sticker, for the lesson's own words

The planted mark is not an invention: it is the two best-known real cases, and the notebook's own word (“watermarks”, 06-1 cell 4). Lapuschkin et al. 2019 (Nature Communications, the Clever Hans paper): a fifth of the PASCAL VOC horse images carry a copyright watermark in the lower left corner; the classifier reads the tag, fails when it is removed, and calls any image with the tag a horse. DeGrave, Janizek & Lee 2021 (Nature Machine Intelligence): COVID-19 chest-radiograph classifiers read laterality markers, arrows and annotations that differed between the positive and negative sources, and their saliency maps said so. Both are the demo in §3 B and C: add the tag to the wrong class and the model follows the tag. A is what remains if the widget teaches only where a sound model looks.
