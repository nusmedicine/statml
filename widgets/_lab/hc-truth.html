Slot 4 · showing a found grouping against a true one

The problem, stated exactly: a cut's group numbers are arbitrary and the truth's are not. cutree numbers the groups in whatever order it meets them, so cluster 1 might as well have been called cluster 3 — a comparison between the two has to be invariant to that relabelling, and colour is not. Paint both from one ramp and a perfect recovery can come out in different colours, which reads as a failure.

Below: the defect, then three ways out, on the real stage and the real engine. Move the seed — the point is what happens across seeds, not on any one.

The defect — two colour scales, one ramp

Two strips over the same twenty samples: the cut on top, the truth below. Same six hues.

At k = 2 the cut recovers the two conditions on essentially every seed, and the strips still disagree on colour most of the time. A student reading colour reads a perfect result as a total failure.

A — one colour scale, the cut as enclosure  (recommended)

Colour carries the truth and nothing else. The cut is drawn as gaps and boxes — the grouping is a grouping, so it gets the channel that means grouping. The reading is one sentence: does each box hold one colour?

Nothing can mis-match, because only one thing is coloured. It is also what pheatmap itself does — annotation_col carries known groups in colour, cutree_cols splits the matrix into blocks — so the student meets this exact division of labour in the tool.

B — matched colours

Keep two coloured strips, but renumber the cut to best-match the truth first, so a colour mismatch is a real mismatch.

Works at k = 2 and breaks above it: push the cut to 4 and two cut groups have to share the truth's one colour, so the strip stops showing that they are different groups. It also quietly teaches that a cluster number means something.

C — agreement only

Drop both identities. Mark each object by whether it landed in a cut group that is pure for its own true group.

Unambiguous and permutation-invariant, but it answers a yes/no where the interesting question is which group got split into what. It throws away the thing the four blocks are for.

D — the number, whichever picture wins

Whatever the figure does, the readout should state the comparison as a number, because a number is permutation-invariant by construction and the eye's verdict is not. A contingency table of true groups against cut groups is the honest form; one line of it fits a stat tile.

Purity has a known failure — it goes to 1 if you ask for enough clusters — so the tile has to print k beside it, which this widget's readout already does.
