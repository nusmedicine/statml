§3 · The base study's size — the lever the widget is missing

The draft's base study is 1,500 simulated people, and that caps every curve after it. A real base is a FILE OF SUMMARY STATISTICS — one β̂ and one P a SNP — so the widget can have any base size for free: for SNP j with allele frequency pj and true effect βj on the standardised genotype, β̂j ~ N(βj, sej²) with sej = √((1 − βj²)/n) ≈ 1/√n, and Pj from the two-sided normal tail. The approximation is that a SNP's own variance explained is treated as its only departure from 1 and that the allele frequency is the base study's true one rather than its realised one. The widget's score is built on the raw allele count, so both β̂ and se are divided by √(2pj(1 − pj)). The first measured line below is the check against the per-person route at n = 1,500.

§5 · Page 5a — calibration

The question the four pages withhold an answer to: a percentile is not a risk. A disease is simulated from the trait as a liability with a threshold at a Prevalence; a logistic model of the outcome on the standardised score is fitted in a base population of 3,000 drawn at the base study's own allele frequencies — not in the target, which is the whole point — and applied to the target with the base population's own mean and SD. The plot is mean predicted risk against the observed fraction by decile of predicted risk, the diagonal as the reference, with a 95% interval on each observed fraction because a decile of 319 people holds 32 of them. A one panel under the existing Target population control; B the three populations side by side.

§6 · Page 5b — stratification

Not an arbitrary top decile: the cut is the percentile at which calibrated absolute risk crosses a threshold somebody already acts on. Predicted risk against score percentile in the target, a Risk threshold as the reference line, the crossing percentile marked and the number of the 319 above it. A the figure alone; B the figure beside 5a's calibration plot on one stage, so the check and the consequence are read together.
