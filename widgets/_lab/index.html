The harness

Not comparisons — the tools that hash a widget and prove it still renders identically.

  • The rendering fingerprint The suite. Hashes every baselined state twice — px over the canvas, tx over the figure's text — and fails a state carrying only one. Auto-runs on load; never click Run.
  • Hash new fingerprint states Records hashes for states not yet in the baseline, without re-running the whole suite.
  • Widget 23 · hashing its states Reproduces existing baseline hashes first, to prove its copy of the harness has not drifted, before printing anything new.

  • What the MCMC tab should show Settled: B, and hollow rings. For widget 9. MCMC does not compute the normalising constant — it avoids it, because P(counts) cancels out of the acceptance ratio. Also three ways to show the counts a lead has dealt but Step has not reached: rings won, because at n = 60 a faint dot is indistinguishable from a solid one in the same column.
  • Where do the numbers come from? Settled: scope A, in two stages. For widget 11. Stage 1 asks how did these numbers get made? and plays the notebook's four count distributions as generative mechanisms — in two pairs, where only one thing changes between the panels of a pair: fixed chance vs moving chance, fixed rate vs redrawn rate. Stage 2 asks which one is my data? as a four-question decision tree, none of the questions about the shape of a histogram, landing on the distribution's R code and figure with d/p/q/r read off it. Also carries the measurement that fixed the framing: Poisson against negative binomial separates 3.5× at the notebook's own numbers, binomial against hypergeometric only 5% — so the second pair's lesson is “the chance moves as the pool empties”, not “without replacement is narrower”.
