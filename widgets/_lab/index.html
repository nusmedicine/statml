Design lab

Side-by-side comparisons for decisions that are easier to settle by looking than by arguing. Not part of the deployed site — the build skips underscore-prefixed directories.

  • Drop paths Four candidate animation paths, with dots at equal time intervals so pacing shows and not just shape.
  • Control styles Dropdown vs discrete slider vs segmented buttons for a small ordered choice, plus a continuous-zoom alternative.
  • Shuffle visuals For widget 5. Do the labels move, or do the dots? Same data and same shuffles in lockstep, with value guides so you can check whether anything actually moved.
  • The likelihood figure For widget 8. Whether a likelihood curve can be built one candidate at a time the way every pile in this collection is built — and whether that reads on counts or only on a normal. The 2-D surface it also sketches was built and then cut — see the next card.
  • Widget 8, read by a biology MSc Three readings of the readout — as shipped, plain, and minimal — rendered from the widget's real numbers, plus a verdict on every string in it. The reader is comfortable with counts and means and not with 10⁻¹¹⁰.
  • Two curves, then the surface Records a design that was cut. Widget 8 is one parameter; the two-parameter stages were built and removed because each press swept one mean and all the spreads, so one curve grew a dot at a time while the other was complete after the first press. Kept for widget 9: three layouts for a surface with its two marginals, and the finding that two upright curves side by side make the worse-determined parameter look better determined.
  • The null and the p-value For widget 5. Tests whether “a dot that lands in a tail stays lit” makes p countable rather than an abstract area. A real effect beside no effect at all, so the false positive is one click away.
