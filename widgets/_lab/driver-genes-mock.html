0 · What the measurement found

1 · Page 1, One gene: cell 12's five steps on one gene

An oncogene shaped like PIK3CA in the lesson's file: 1,068 residues, 359 mutations, its hotspots where PIK3CA's are. Each step is one press of Step. The top panel is the whole protein as a lollipop (a round head is missense, a square head truncating); the bottom panel is a close-up of one cluster, because the rule that merges residues works at 5 residues, which is 2 pixels on the whole protein.

2 · The four genes the page offers, each at its last step

Each is simulated with the length, count, hotspots and truncating share of a gene in the lesson's file, counted once per tumour; _lab/driver-genes-measure.mjs §5 checks each shape's median score is within 0.1 of its gene's. The small oncogene has 26 mutations and the oncogene 359.

3 · Page 2, The cohort: cell 18's plot, with each gene's kind known

One simulated cohort: genes, nine of them drivers shaped like the lesson's (PIK3CA, AKT1, KRAS; TP53, CDH1, GATA3, MAP3K1, PTEN, NF1), the rest passengers whose counts and residues are calibrated to the file. Axes as plotOncodrive(useFraction = TRUE): the fraction of mutations in clusters across, −log10 FDR up, size the number of clusters. Colour is the kind the simulation gave each gene; a ring is a call at FDR ≤ 0.05. The same cohort, read two ways: B is A with each mutation recorded a second time at the rate the lesson's MAF repeats records.

4 · A count test beside the clustering score?

The notebook runs none. maftools' own (pvalMethod = "poisson") is two-sided with the cluster count as a covariate, so 19 of its 32 calls on the lesson's file are genes with fewer mutations than expected. B is a one-sided test of our own: is a gene's count above what its length predicts at the cohort's rate? On the lesson's file, counted once, it calls 207 of 4,566 genes — every suppressor, and long genes such as RYR2, LRP2, FAT3 and CSMD3.
