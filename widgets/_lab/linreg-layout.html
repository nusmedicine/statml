2 · The objective, as a live formula

Unicode, drawn on the canvas — no KaTeX, because the repo has no runtime dependencies and no build step. A term greys out when its dial is at zero, so the formula reads as the objective you are actually minimising. That may make the four-cell key redundant: with α1 dim the line already IS ridge.

3 · Picking a pair — 13 features is 78 pairs

The correlation matrix is what sets the contour shape, so a selector that shows it is a control carrying an idea rather than a way to choose. Click any cell.

C1 · Clickable correlation matrix

Every pair reachable, and the grid teaches the thing the choice controls: dark is correlated, so dark cells are the elongated contours. Needs canvas hit-testing, which no widget in the repo has yet — that is core work.

C2 · Two dropdowns, or one with Abdomen fixed

No core work at all. Twelve partners against Abdomen already span r = 0.09 to 0.92, which is the whole range the lesson needs; the other 66 pairs add coverage but no new idea. The plane's x-axis also stops moving.

4 · Layout D, carrying the fitted equation

Not the objective — the model, with the weights in it. It reserves three lines because thirteen terms need three at 11 px, and the whole point is watching it collapse to one. Raise α1 and it shortens; raise α2 instead and it stays three lines for ever.

Rendered as real DOM at the rail's real width (minmax(250px, 300px)), with the page's own tokens. Drag the α dials at the top and watch the live cell move. The question is whether the grid earns its room here, or whether one live line of text says the same thing.
