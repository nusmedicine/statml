What the real cells said, and what the stage had to be rebuilt to carry. (1) mt% here is a property of the SAMPLE, not of a cell type: inside HB17_background every marker group sits at 12–15%, and the other background liver — same tissue, other patient — sits at 0.1%. (2) Inside the three clean samples mt% falls as the count rises (r = −0.40 to −0.70), which is the dying cell keeping its mitochondria; in the hot sample it is flat across every count decile (13.5% in the lowest, 15.8% in the highest), which is ambient mitochondrial RNA in every droplet. So the stage has both mechanisms and they are separate levers. (3) nCount and nFeature are one measurement twice: r = 0.95–0.99, and 1,199 of the 1,434 cells either rule removes fail BOTH. Here nFeature is not drawn — it is how many genes are seen when the droplet's molecules are drawn from its own profile, and the 89% overlap falls out. (4) The lesson's mt% rule removes 85% of one sample; at mt% < 20 it would remove 29%. The stage reproduces that curve to within a few points: 7 / 42 / 71 / 82% kept at mt% < 10 / 15 / 20 / 25 against the real 15 / 42 / 71 / 85%.

5 · Truth — what the removed droplets actually held

The stage knows: a good cell, a dying cell, an empty droplet of ambient RNA, or two cells in one droplet. So the filter can be read as the claim it is. A: the same scatter, each droplet coloured by what it holds, with the thresholds on it. B: the map — each droplet placed by the profile its own molecules measured, so a shallow droplet lands away from its type, a doublet lands halfway between the two it holds, and an empty droplet lands in the middle. Position is the cell type; colour is free to carry the state.
