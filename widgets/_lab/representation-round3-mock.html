1 · Masked: what the model fills in

You read it right. The model is given the masked input (half the pixels set to 0) and is scored only on the hidden pixels. Its output is a whole image, though, and the strip drew all of it, so the visible pixels the model was given appear again as if it had rebuilt them. Drawn as inpainting, the visible pixels stay as they were given and only the hidden ones come from the model. (This is pixel masking, 50% of pixels at random, as cell 61 does; the hidden pixels are scattered, so each one has visible neighbours, which is why the prediction is good after a few hundred batches.)

Recommend A: the third box is the masked input with its hidden pixels filled in from the prediction, the filled pixels in ink and the given ones in light ink, so the eye sees exactly what the model supplied. Its error is on the filled pixels only, as the loss is.

2 · Contrastive: the dissimilar pairs, by hovering

A hover over any cell of the matrix shows its two views side by side with their similarity at the checkpoint on screen; a cell off the diagonal is a dissimilar pair (views of two different images, here two different digits). A click on a row picks that digit, as a click on a map point does.

Recommend the hover card as drawn: view 1 of the row's digit, view 2 of the column's, "similarity" and the value; on the diagonal it says "matching views", off it "different images".

3 · The embedding organising itself during Train

Recommend B: during Train the right-hand map shows the current checkpoint's UMAP (titled "after 50 batches" and so on), switching at each checkpoint and ending on the trained map; the left stays untrained. Each frame is the notebook's own UMAP, so it can be read on its own, and nothing moves across the frame. The layouts are independent, so the clusters change place from one checkpoint to the next; the reading is how gathered the digits are, not where. Two things the frames show that the page would say: on Masked and Contrastive the untrained embedding is already sorted, the first batches scramble it (silhouette near 0 at 20–100 batches) and training then sorts it further; and these maps are laid out from the 2,000 drawn digits, so the last frame is close to, not identical to, the round-2 trained map (laid out from all 10,000): the widget would use the 2,000-digit layout for both.
