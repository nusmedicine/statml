94's split diagram, set apart from the representation · mock

His note on the Interactions page: "I don't see some visual separator … I get confused about the diagram about splitting … options to maintain some conceptual and visual separation to distinguish between the representation and the training part." The strip sits straight under the status line, in the same ink and the same type, so it reads as one more part of the figure above it. Four ways to set it apart, from the lightest to a page of its own; each drawn as the foot of the Interactions page at its 549px. What stays on the example pages in every option: the split as the tables store it (train_mask and test_mask, edge_label_index, the table's split column), since those are fields of the data, i.e. representation.

A · A rule and a heading

A full-width rule after the status line, then a heading that names the change of subject: Training · what a split holds out. The least change; the strip still shares the figure's surface.

B · A band of its own

The strip on a tinted band the width of the canvas, with the heading inside, separated from the figure by space. It reads as a box beside the figure rather than part of it, the way an aside does in a text.

C · Behind a toggle

A rail checkbox Split diagram under the page's own controls, off at first; on, the band of B appears. The figure alone until the reader asks. Costs a control on three pages, and a diagram nobody opens teaches nothing.

D · A page of its own, under a Training heading

The example pages lose the strip; the rail gains a third heading, Training, over one button Splits. The page has room to draw each split on that example's own data: the twelve patients with four ringed, the ten proteins with two edges dashed, the six molecules in a train box and a test box, each with the field that stores it. A click on a panel opens that example page. The rail then reads Basics · Examples · Training, which is the separation itself.

Recommended: D. It makes the separation structural rather than decorative: representation on the example pages, the split on a page under Training, and the rail saying so. It also removes a strip repeated on three pages, and gives the comparison room to use each example's real data instead of a stand-in graph. If a page is too much, B: the band is the clearest visual separation that keeps the strip where it is.
