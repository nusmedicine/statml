Slot 4 candidate · hierarchical-clustering — the stage, the cut, and what the tree already knew

PHM5003 05 - Introduction to High Throughput Data / 08 - Hierarchical Clustering. Everything below is drawn from widgets/hierarchical-clustering/model.js — the engine that would ship, imported and not copied. It is verified against R's own hclust: node widgets/_lab/hc-verify.mjs, 4200 comparisons over 35 stages and five linkages, heights to 1e-9 and cuts exactly. node widgets/_lab/hc-measure.mjs prints every number quoted here.

The claim, after measuring — it is not the one the catalogue proposed. The plan said "pure noise produces a handsome dendrogram". It does not: on this stage real groups score a final-merge gap of 4.20 and noise 1.39, and 0 of 400 noise runs reached the 10th percentile of the real ones. The tree is honest. What noise produces is a handsome cut — ask Ward for two clusters in pure noise and 56% of the time it hands back a balanced split that looks exactly like a finding. So the widget is not about being fooled by a picture. It is about the picture carrying the evidence and the answer throwing it away.

§1 · THE STAGE — why not the notebook's own

Cell 15 is twenty log-fold-changes in one dimension, two groups at ±1.5 with sd 0.5. Measured in R: all five linkages return the identical 10/10 split, 20 of 20 correct. They first disagree at k = 3. On that stage a linkage control does nothing at the cut anyone looks at, and a distance control cannot do anything at all — in 1-D every distance is |x − y| up to a monotone transform. Two dimensions fixes both: the three linkages agree on only 16% of 2-D noise runs against 36% in 1-D.

ASK 1 — the layout. A and B are the same data and the same tree, differing only in where the dendrogram sits. A puts it to the right, so a merge is a horizontal move from the scatter; B puts it below, so the leaves line up under the points they belong to. B costs height, which this repo pays for in a taller widget. Which?

§2 · THE MERGE SEQUENCE IS THE ANIMATION

shown is the number of merges performed: N singletons, merge the closest pair, repeat. The dendrogram grows one join upward per step at the merge height, and the scatter draws a link between the two clusters that just joined. Four moments from one run below.

The animation and the cut are deliberately separate. After m merges you have N − m clusters, so "stop the animation at step 18" and "cut the finished tree at k = 2" reach the same partition — and folding them into one control would blur exactly the distinction the widget teaches. The tree is evidence; the cut is a choice laid over it. So shown builds the tree and k cuts it, and k is a display parameter that does not reset the animation.

ASK 2 — one control or two. Above is two: build the whole tree, then move the cut. The alternative is one — shown alone, with "where you stopped" being the answer. One control is simpler and makes the merge count and the cluster count visibly the same number; two matches hclust then cutree, which is what the notebook writes, and lets a reader move the cut on a finished tree without rebuilding it. I recommend two.

§3 · THE SEPARATION SLIDER — the whole argument on one control

Every column is the same seed and the same linkage; only the separation moves. Read the bottom row: the cut keeps answering long after there is anything to answer about.

§4 · THE FAILING CASE — pure noise, and a cut that looks like a finding

Separation 0. One Gaussian, no groups at all. Below is the seed measured as the most convincing of 200 — a balanced split with the highest gap noise produced — beside two ordinary noise seeds. All three are answers to "give me two clusters" and none of them is a finding.

ASK 3 — how hard should the readout push? Three registers, in rising order of how much the widget does for the reader:
(a) print the gap and nothing else — gap 1.39 — and let them learn to read it;
(b) print the gap beside what real groups score — gap 1.39 · real groups here score 3.3-5.2;
(c) print a verdict — this cut is not supported.
Principle 2.11 says a widget claims only what is on screen, which rules out (c) on a single draw. I recommend (b): it is a comparison, not a verdict, and the reader can check it by moving the slider. But it does put a number on screen that this stage alone does not show.

§5 · THE LINKAGE — inert on real groups, decisive on noise

Top row: separation 3, the three linkages the notebook teaches. They agree — 94% of seeds agree at this separation, so a reader moving this control on real groups sees almost nothing, which is the honest lesson and not a defect. Bottom row: separation 0, where they agree on only 16% of seeds. The control does nothing when there is something to find, and everything when there is not.

§6 · ACT 2 — the notebook's own heatmap, and the box around nothing

Cells 34-43: 50 genes by 20 samples, four planted blocks of ten, and genes 41-50 with nothing added at all. Cell 41 calls cutree_rows = 5 and says nothing about the fifth box. Measured in R on the notebook's own seed: four boxes are the planted blocks and the fifth is nine unstructured genes. Ask for eight and two boxes are pure noise. The row dendrogram's top merge heights are 16.29, 13.01, 12.43, 10.70, then 8.95, 8.57, 8.52, 8.28 — it falls off a cliff after the fourth merge and the boxes do not care.

ASK 4 — act 2's control. The sketch above moves cutree_rows only. The notebook also moves cutree_cols, where the same thing happens on the sample side: a stage with exactly two groups subdivides into 3, 4, 5, 6 tidy boxes, every one still inside a single condition. One cut control or two? Two doubles the argument at no conceptual cost, but it is a second slider on a panel that already carries the gate.
