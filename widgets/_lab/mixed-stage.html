Widget 32 · mixed-model — the stage, drawn before it is argued

The misconception (catalogue slot 6, Hurlbert 1984): 500 rows are 500 observations. Measured first (node widgets/_lab/mixed-measure.mjs, 59 checks — the engine reproduces every printed number of 05-07's BP tables to the digit, lm at 1e-8 against R's own fit on the exactly-regenerated data; and mixed-design.mjs, the sweeps every claim below cites). The notebook's own story, confirmed on the regenerated data: the medication does NOTHING, yet lm on 500 rows reports −3.4 [−5.3, −1.5]; lmer with the patient as a random effect reads −3.6 [−7.5, 0.2] — no effect, the correct conclusion. §3 · The dials, drawn from the measured sweeps

D — more visits of the same patients. perPatient 1 → 5 → 20 at 100 patients: the lm interval tightens toward certainty about an effect that does not exist (measured half-width 4.5 → 2.0 → 1.2, false-positive 5% → 38% → 57%) while the honest interval barely moves (~4.1 throughout). One sentence of caption: more rows, same evidence. E — the 500-row trade. 500×1, 100×5, 25×20 all print "Num.Obs. 500"; lm cannot tell them apart (half-width ≈ 2.0–2.5 everywhere) while the honest interval reads 2.0 / 4.1 / 8.4 — the information is the patients, and the widget's two sliders make that a thing you DO rather than read. At 500×1 the two fits agree — the mixed model costs nothing when rows really are independent.

§4 · The families tab candidate — the SNP forest, lm vs lmer

The notebook's second example is a different hierarchy (patients inside families) and the same failure. F: ten SNP effects as a paired forest — the lm estimate and the lmer estimate per SNP, zero line reference, causal truth marked. On this seed lm flags SNPs 2, 4 and 5; lmer keeps only 5. Measured over 50 seeds at family SD 5: lm averages 2.5 false SNPs of 9 and misses the real one 22% of the time; lmer: 0.64 false, finds SNP5 on 50/50 — modeling the families GAINS power, it does not just cost false positives.
