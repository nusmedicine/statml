multiple-testing — two candidate panels, two layouts

Same computation behind every panel: 20,000 genes, 100 of them real at 2.0 SD, n = 12. The question is not whether the panels are correct, it is which arrangement a room can read. Not deployed; _lab/ is excluded from the site.

A · side by side

Half width each. Compact, but the threshold panel loses horizontal room — and horizontal room is exactly what its rank axis needs.

B · stacked, full width

Each panel gets the full width. Costs vertical space, which on a projector is the scarcer of the two.

D · three stacked carpets, CURRENT linear scale

Same histogram three times. Within each bar, the red part is the share of those genes the rule actually calls. Bonferroni cuts at 2.5e-6, which is the same pixel as zero on this axis — so it cannot be a vertical line here, and the only way it shows at all is as a fraction of the first bar's HEIGHT.

E · the same three, log p-axis

Now each rule is a clean vertical cut and everything left of it is called. Legible, at the cost of an axis students must be taught to read.

C · overlap only, as a wide short strip

The effect-size panel does not need height — it is two curves and a shaded overlap. A short full-width strip may buy most of its value for a third of the space, leaving the thresholds panel a full-height slot of its own.
