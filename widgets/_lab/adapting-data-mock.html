The misreadings this leaves open (two of them yours, this week)

  • "From scratch is trained on different (or more) data." You asked whether from scratch on Outcome trained on Drug error's notes. The fair comparison — same labelled notes, different start — is the widget's whole design and is not on the page.
  • "Pretraining needs labels too." The point of self-supervision is that the 20,000 notes carry no label: the hidden word is the target.
  • "The pretrained model knows the task." It was never shown a label for any task; that is why the head alone fails on Drug error.

What the field draws

The standard picture of adapting is two stages on a line: (1) self-supervised pretraining on a large unlabelled corpus, then (2) supervised training on a small labelled set for one task (Jay Alammar's Illustrated BERT, the Hugging Face course's transfer-learning figure). From scratch is stage 2 alone. Widget 84 already animates stage 1 (the masked sentence, the [MASK] row scored), so 85 needs it as a reminder with one example, not a second animation.

Principles the options are judged on

  • Contrast in a fixed place (consistency): the same strip at the same position on every page, so switching From scratch ↔ Full changes one box and the eye catches it.
  • Concrete before abstract (worked example, dual coding): one real masked note beside one real labelled note says "no label vs label" faster than the words "self-supervised".
  • Show quantities: 20,000 unlabelled against 1,024 labelled is the economic argument for pretraining.
  • Spend nothing twice; keep the figure first (the map and the curve are the page): one band, no animation, no new control; the LoRA page must stay inside 1,200 px.
