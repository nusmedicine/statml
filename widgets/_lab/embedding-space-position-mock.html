embedding-space · the Position page's middle panel

The sinusoid and the rotation both turn with position, and as dials they look the same. Three ways to tell them apart, each drawn for the sixteen-token sentence at E = 8 (four pairs) on seeded N(0, 1) embedding rows. The question is what the middle panel shows for each encoding; the top and bottom panels (x, and x̃) stay as they are.

A · the sinusoid as curves, the rotation as the vector turned

Sinusoidal: the position rows ARE samples of sine and cosine curves, so draw the curves across the token axis and mark where each token reads them: pair 0 turns a radian a position, pair 1 a tenth of that, and so on down; the heatmap beneath is those samples. Rotary: nothing is read off a curve; the token's own pair of numbers is a small arrow, and the position turns it: the arrow before (grey) and after (colour), with the arc it swept.

B · both as dials, but the rotation's dial carries the vector

The sinusoid's dial is a unit-circle point (the angle alone); the rotation's dial is the embedding pair itself as an arrow of its own length, turned. Smaller change, smaller difference.

C · the sinusoid as curves; the rotation as the whole row's pairs turned

As A for the sinusoid. For the rotation, every pair of the row at once: four arrows a token (pair 0 to 3), each turned by its own slower angle, so the reader sees that one row is turned four ways and the slow pairs barely move.
