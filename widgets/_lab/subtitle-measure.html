How wide should a subtitle be?

The question that produced this page: why not let the subtitle go full width, instead of stopping at 40% and leaving the right side empty? It is the obvious instinct and the cap does look arbitrary. The answer is not a rule, it is a number — characters per line — so this page shows the real copy at each width and lets you read it.

Comfortable reading is 45–75 characters per line. Below 45 the eye jumps too often; above 75 the return sweep starts landing on the wrong line and you re-read. The red marks below each block are where 45 and 75 fall in this exact font.

Each block below is drawn at its true pixel width, not at whatever your window allows — so the comparison holds even on a narrow screen. The first block scrolls sideways if your window is under ~1150px, which is itself worth noticing: that is the line you would be asking a student to read.

What this settles, and it is not what I first said. Full width is out — 196 characters is 2.6x the maximum, and the eye cannot find the next line. But the shipping measure is also out: 82 characters, seven past the limit. The cap is not too narrow, it is too wide.

So there are two independent levers, and they do different jobs:

  • Width sets characters per line. To reach 75 the cap has to come down to about 429px — narrower than today, not wider.
  • Copy length sets the number of lines. 63 words wraps to five; 47 words wraps to four. It does nothing at all to the line length.

Which means the empty space to the right is not a problem stretching text can solve — stretching makes it less readable, not more. It is solved by shortening the copy so the block is compact enough to read as a deliberate deck rather than as a column that ran out. The last block is both levers pulled: 47 words at 429px, four lines, 75 characters, inside the band.
