§1 · The scatter's x-axis: both at once, or the reader's choice

Age's relationship with sysBP (r = 0.37, the stronger of the two) is currently never SEEN. A shows both marginals at once — 05-02's own ggpairs move — two half-width panels, no control, but neither panel can host the FWL slide or the plane family well at 250px. B gives the scatter an x-axis control (x: BMI · age): one full panel, and everything already built comes along — the plane family redraws over age (lines per BMI band), and the FWL slide generalises to "the OTHER covariate removed". B is the recommendation: one more segmented, nothing else new.

§2 · The twins, clickable APART — each is fine alone; together they break

Weight becomes a third pill (and DAG node) on the Collinearity tab, so the reader composes the model freely — and the lesson sharpens: BMI alone works, weight alone works too (it is a real predictor — measured below), and only BOTH together break. "Adding a variable that carries the same information" becomes an act the reader performs, not a demonstration they watch.

§3 · VIF, drawn instead of defined

"Regress the covariate on the rest" IS a picture: the covariate against what the others predict for it. For weight, the others predict it tightly — the cloud hugs the diagonal, R² is high, and VIF = 1 ⁄ (1 − R²) is large. For age, the others predict almost nothing — the cloud is a blob, R² ≈ 0, VIF ≈ 1. The two panels side by side teach the definition without a sentence; the arithmetic line under each connects the picture to the bar. In the widget: click a VIF bar (regions) to put that covariate's picture in the panel — the bar and its explanation reading each other.
