count-normalization · the 2,000-gene panel three ways

His question, 2026-09-22: is the histogram the right way to visualise this? would a boxplot work? The same 2,000 genes (lengths differ, sample B at 3×) drawn three ways, in three states across each row: raw counts with gene 5's group up; TPM with gene 5's group up; the size factor with most genes up. Every number is computed on this page. Blue is the unchanged genes, violet the changed; the grey line is the truth for an unchanged gene and the violet line the truth for a changed one. 550 × 156 px, the panel's own size.

A · the histogram of log2(B ÷ A), as now

Each gene becomes one number, its ratio between the samples. Up shows only as "right of the truth line", which is what made the size-factor state read as reversed.

B · boxes of the values themselves: unchanged and changed genes, in each sample

Four boxes on a log axis: the unchanged genes in A and in B, then the changed genes in A and in B. B above A, or level with it, is seen directly; the truth is the two unchanged boxes level and the changed pair eight times apart (three on the log2 axis). The medians are printed. This is widget 39's own figure, one box per sample, split by whether the gene changed.

C · the MA plot, as 01-2 draws it (plotMA)

The ratio against the mean, one dot per gene. The same ratio as A, spread out by expression; it is the lesson's own figure and 78's, so a reader meets it again there.

The call

The panel B · the boxes (recommended: no ratio, the direction is seen, and it is the figure 39 already taught)  |  A · the histogram as now  |  C · the MA plot  |  B over A, both (312px, the widget grows)

Under B, the tile the unchanged genes' median in B ÷ A stays the number (0 on the log2 axis is the truth), read off the two blue boxes' medians instead of the histogram.
