Widget 27 · lm-least-squares — the line, the sum, the surface

05-01's own pedagogy: pick b₀ and b₁ by hand, watch the sum of squares, then see the fitted line as the minimum of a surface. Everything below is drawn on the real data (lm-data.js, the notebooks' own Framingham frame, n = 3547) with the reader's line at the notebook's own hand-picked b₀ = 70, b₁ = 2 (cell 11) — SS 1,762,150, 25% above the minimum. The fitted answer is b₀ = 87.07, b₁ = 1.72, verified against every stored output (lm-measure.mjs, 18 checks).

The measured constraint every candidate answers to: the surface spans 44.5× on the notebook's grid, but the whole teaching range is the bottom 10% — the flat mean line (b₁ = 0) is only 10.6% above the minimum, and that gap IS R². A linear scale washes the teaching range into one band (§2 shows it). The valley is a 13:1 diagonal trench, not a bowl.

§1 · Stage composition — what the 550px canvas holds

A scatter alone, SS as a meter strip — the surface never drawn; B scatter beside the surface, "you are here" linked — one glance, smaller panels; C scatter above the surface — both get width, the widget gets taller (~500px, cf. the repo's usual ~330). All surface panels here use the capped-relative scale that §2 argues for.

§2 · The surface's scale — the design problem, drawn

Same grid as the notebook (b₀ 0–150, b₁ 0–5), four treatments. Linear is the notebook's own look and the wash is the measurement made visible: everything below ~10× the minimum is one colour. Log recovers the trench. Capped at 3× spends the whole ramp on the teaching range (every line a reader would actually hold sits under 3× the minimum). Banded is capped-with-levels — contour-like without pretending precision. The minimum is crossed, the reader's line dotted.

§3 · The misfit on the scatter — what the SS number points at

The notebook's diagram draws the vertical differences. At n = 3547: A no marks (the SS tile carries it alone); B every residual as a faint segment — misfit becomes a wash whose depth varies; C a 60-patient systematic subsample wearing literal squares — honest about squared, but the squares are screen-square (true squares only under equal axis scaling) and the sum on screen is no longer the SS the tile reports. 2.11 (visible-data-only claims) leans against C.

§4 · What "Fit" reveals — the walk downhill

The notebook's optim is the argmin move. Two candidate reveals: snap — the minimum appears, the line eases to it (widget 12's mechanism, one beat); walk — a descent path drawn step by step, the line swinging as the point slides. The path below is coordinate descent from the notebook's own start (0, 0): b₀ then b₁, each an exact one-dimensional minimisation. It reaches the trench in two moves and then crawls along it — the 13:1 elongation is why, and that crawl is itself the teaching (many near-equally-good lines). The SS-per-step strip underneath is the same walk as a falling curve.

Open interaction questions (for review, not drawn)

How the reader holds the line: b₀/b₁ sliders (honest, names the parameters, no regions needed) vs dragging the line itself (two handles — grab near an end to tilt, middle to shift; needs regions and therefore a hit-driven fingerprint state). Sliders are the notebook's own vocabulary (c(b0, b1)); a drag is the surface metaphor made physical. Both can coexist — drag as shortcut, sliders as record (5.6: a region is a faster way to reach a parameter, never a second state of record). The gate: the surface panel could arrive only after the reader has held a line or two ("Where does my line sit among ALL lines?") — 3.4b's shape. C-composition + gate = A that grows into C.
