2 · A diagram for the split and the training

Two candidates. A · Split places the six in the lesson's three sets: a molecule goes whole to one set, and the split is a list of graphs cut in three, with no mask inside any of them. Why the cut is by scaffold, and what a random cut gets wrong, is 97's subject; here the bar only shows that whole molecules move. B · Batch is what training receives: the DataLoader joins several training molecules into ONE graph with no edge between them — x stacked, each molecule's edge_index shifted by the atoms before it, and a batch vector naming each atom's molecule, so y has one entry a molecule. It is the representation cell 38 prints, and the batch vector is what 95's readout pools over.

Recommended: the table (1), with its split column, and B · Batch as a second view of the Molecule page. The table gives the page the lesson's own starting point and turns y · [1] into a value that changes. The split column already carries the split honestly — whole molecules, three sets, from the lesson's own split — so a bar diagram (A) would repeat it, and its natural next question (why by scaffold) belongs to 97. Batch is the one representation idea the page does not yet have, and it is non-obvious: many molecules become one graph, and only the batch vector keeps them apart. The view would batch the training rows the reader picks, two or three, with Next bond unchanged on the single view.
