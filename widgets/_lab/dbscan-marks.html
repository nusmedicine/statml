Widget 24 draws four states of a dot, and the tokens file has names for two

Cell 60 names three kinds of point — core, border, noise — and its diagram draws two: coloured dots for a cluster, a black dot left over for noise. Border points are step 4 of the numbered list and appear nowhere in the picture. The widget also needs a fourth state the diagram does show, in its first panel: nothing decided yet, which is --c-unknown — and that token's own comment scopes it to “not measured yet, not a third outcome”. Noise is exactly the third outcome that comment excludes. Both states are on screen in the same figure, so they cannot share a mark.

And there is a fifth mark competing for the same dot: labels, the true group, which widget 23 draws as a coloured ring outside the dot — deliberately outside, so that what K-Means decided (the fill) and what was really there (the ring) never occupy the same mark. Any grammar that spends the ring on border takes that away, and the two widgets sit side by side on the gallery.

Every figure below is drawn on the real 550 px canvas at the real 4.6 px dot radius, by the shipping engine (../dbscan/model.js), on the two stages that ship. Ink is counted, not estimated: see What each mark costs.

On the default stage — rings, and it has no border or noise to draw

This is the measured consequence of opening on rings, not a property of any candidate below. At every eps that recovers both rings there are 0 border and 0 noise points; over 20 seeds only 5 have any eps where both rings come back and all three kinds are on screen. A ring is a near-uniform chain, so every point crosses into core at the same radius. What each mark costs

Ink is the count of pixels the mark actually paints, at the real radius and at the projector radius, measured off an offscreen canvas rather than reasoned about. A hollow mark carries roughly a third of a filled one's ink and is the first thing to vanish at distance — docs/design-principles.md has the rule and this is the number behind it. Distinct is the share of pixels that differ between that mark and the undecided mark: two marks a reader must tell apart cannot both be grey circles.

What the numbers rule out, and the one that reversed itself

The last two columns above were added after the swatches were looked at, and they changed the answer. The first version of this page measured every mark against undecided, which is the right question for noise and the wrong one for border — border's job is to be told apart from core. Measured, and measured at both radii, because a hollow ring fills in as it shrinks: stroke width is not scale-invariant, so a grammar that separates core from border by fill stops separating them at all on a lecture screen.

Bsurvives, and draws two verdicts every mark ≥ 91% distinct, none under full ink, no channel spent. But border is not drawn at all — B is A with the token added, so step 4 of cell 60 stays unshown. Costs --c-noise.
Esurvives the projector the only candidate whose border/core pair holds as the figure shrinks — 65% → 67%, because size is scale-invariant and stroke width is not. The cost is real and is the reason it was written off first: border is 9 pixels at projector size against core's 23. Nine unambiguous pixels against twenty-two ambiguous ones is the trade.
Gsurvives, and keeps the area added after E's result, to ask whether the scale-invariant channel has to cost area. Border is full size at 45% opacity, so it neither thins nor shrinks. See its row above for whether that holds.

So the shortlist is B, E and G, and the question underneath it has not changed: does the widget draw border points at all? B says cell 60's diagram was right to draw two verdicts and the text's third kind is a footnote. E and G say the text names three kinds and a widget that draws two is teaching the diagram rather than the algorithm.

One thing this page cannot settle

Whether the grammar is learnable is not a pixel count. Candidate C's rule is one sentence — filled means this point built a cluster, hollow means it only joined one, grey means neither — and candidate E's needs two. That is the kind of thing the swatches above are for, and the reason this is a page rather than a table.
