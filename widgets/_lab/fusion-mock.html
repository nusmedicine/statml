Early · one vector before the model, or one verdict per modality

1,000 patients, one gene g and one mutation m; risk = 2.5 g (2m − 1), so the gene raises the risk in mutated patients and lowers it in the rest. 750 train, 250 test. Both models are 10-2b's shapes at a small size (an encoder per modality, 4-dimensional embeddings), Adam 1e-2, weight decay 1e-2, 300 full-batch epochs. The first build (400 patients, no weight decay) overfit: a bump and a plateau on concatenation's curves and a wiggle on weighted averaging's that read as signal. Weighted averaging's flat line is the right answer, not a failure to train: with the flip symmetric, P(outcome | g) = 0.5 for every g and P(outcome | m) = 0.5 for both statuses, so the best each single-modality verdict can do is the base rate.

Measured and not drawn: the gated model reaches with these settings and 0.75 in the first build: the gate multiplies the two verdicts, so it can learn the flip, and whether it does depends on the training settings. On his call the page compares the two that differ in where the modalities meet.

Late · step 1, one α for all patients; step 2, the gate

1,000 patients (750 train). Expression is five genes (their mean is the risk signature) and five driver mutations; both modalities informative. In 30% of patients the expression assay failed: every gene reads near background (−2 in standardized units). Each modality's verdict is the log-odds from a logistic regression on that modality alone. The expression model is fitted on the training patients whose assay worked, a model built on good samples and then applied to every patient, so it calls the failed patients low risk with confidence (log-odds about −4.6, whatever their outcome). Fitted on every training patient instead, its slope flattened (measured verdicts −0.3 to +1, failed −0.6) and the gate only matched one α (0.76 against 0.75). Step 1: fused = α × expression + (1 − α) × mutations, α the student's slider; the learned α minimizes the training log-loss with both verdicts held fixed. Step 2: the gate, a small network on both modalities' measurements (5 genes + 5 mutations → 16 → 1, sigmoid), trained with the two verdicts held fixed, so the only thing that changes between the steps is α. Weight decay 1e-2: without it the gate's α for measured patients spread from 0 to 1.

Why a failed assay that reads low, not a blank row: blank rows were tried first and the gate hardly moved (α 0.44 against 0.49). A blank row already gets an expression verdict near 0, so its weight barely changes the loss. A gate earns its place where one modality is confidently wrong for patients the gate can recognise, here a whole profile at background. (10-2b's own network, trained jointly on blank rows, does separate them, 0.84 against 0.46: the two set-ups differ, and the catalogue has both.)
