count-normalization · within and between, from the same six genes

His questions, 2026-09-22: the histogram can demonstrate between comparisons; can it also do within? The histogram is a simulation of 2,000 genes and the table shows six — could be confusing. And: I have to mentally convert B/A with log2; show the log2 fold change too. What the sources do. The HBC page draws three toys: depth as 2 samples × 3 genes, length as 2 genes in one sample, composition as 2 samples × a few genes with one gene taking the reads. StatQuest and Pimentel compute RPKM and TPM by hand on a 3-gene × 3-sample table and show that TPM's columns sum to the same total. None of them uses more genes than a table can hold; the 2,000 are this widget's addition. So every candidate below is drawn from the SAME six genes the table prints, in the same unit, and the 2,000 appear only in D, which answers the within question for a large panel. Four states across each row: raw counts, TPM, the size factor, the size factor per kb (depth 3×, lengths differ, gene 5 up 8×). Every number is computed here. Blue is an unchanged gene, violet gene 5; grey lines are truths. Every ratio carries its log2.

A · the histogram of 2,000 genes, as now — between only

One number per gene, B ÷ A. It cannot show a within comparison: a within reading is two GENES in one sample, and a ratio per gene has already divided one sample by the other. Its genes are not the table's.

B · the six genes as a slope chart: a dot per gene in each sample, a line joining them

Two columns, sample A and sample B, the value on a log2 axis. Within is the height of the dots in ONE column: the truth is every unchanged gene at one height, since all six have the same expression per kilobase, so a unit that divides out length stacks the five blue dots on the grey line. Between is the slope of a gene's line: the truth is flat for an unchanged gene and +3 for gene 5, so a unit that compares between samples draws five flat lines. The two readings the table prints are the two marked pairs, with their log2.

C · the six genes as paired bars: A and B side by side for each gene

The figure people draw with TPM for a few genes of interest. Within is reading the A bars across the genes against the grey line (the unchanged level); between is the two bars of one gene. On a log2 axis so gene 5's 8× does not flatten the rest.

D · the 2,000 genes read WITHIN a sample: the value against the gene's length, in sample A

The within version of the histogram, for the question. One dot per gene, sample A only; the fitted slope is printed. A unit that carries length slopes up at 1 (a 2× longer gene reads 2× higher); one that divides it out is flat. The genes' expression per kilobase varies, so the cloud is wide; only the slope is the reading. It answers the question but adds a third data set and a regression to the stage.

The call

Recommended: B in place of the histogram, and the 2,000 genes gone from the stage. The table, the piles and B are then one data set, the six genes, read three ways in one unit; B shows both comparisons at once and the truth for each is a line the dots either sit on or do not. The histogram's job (the toy holds at scale, with noise) can go behind a gate, "Show 2,000 genes", if you want to keep it reachable, or go entirely.
