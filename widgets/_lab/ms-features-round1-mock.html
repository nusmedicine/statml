Slot 93 · ms-features · round 1: what each step shows, hover and tweens

Your questions: what the Peaks page shows, how to make every step clearer, and where hover and tweens would help. Every frame below is drawn from the draft's own engine (widgets/ms-features/engine.js, the same traces and features) at the draft's canvas width, 610px. Each arm is drawn as the moments of its press. Tweens follow the rules we already use: only movement is eased, appearances switch at once, and a mover keeps to its own lane. The blue-ruled boxes in §5 are your calls.

1 · Peaks — what the draft does not show

The concept: an LC-MS run is intensity everywhere on an m/z × RT plane. Most of it is noise, with bright spots where a metabolite reaches the detector. Detection picks out the spots that stand at least 3 noise SDs above the baseline. Integration turns each spot into one number, its area, which becomes a row of the table. The draft shows none of the first part: its map starts empty and dots appear. The map and the trace below it are on different RT scales, with nothing joining them. And S/N 3 is a dashed line, while the ratio itself is never drawn.

B recommended. (1) The map is raw intensity, noise and spots on the --c-magnitude ramp, before anything is pressed. So the page opens on the data, and Detect has something to find. (2) A zoom box on the map, with connector lines down to the trace, makes the trace visibly that box's row. (3) The trace draws the noise band (±1 SD) and, at each detected peak, a bracket from the baseline to the apex labelled in noise SDs: S/N shown as a length. (4) Integrate ends in a table row per peak; the tween moves each area's number down into its row, in a lane of its own beside the trace. The hover shows what is under the pointer: a spot on the map (its name, m/z, RT and the file's area) or a point on the trace (RT, intensity, S/N).

2 · Alignment — the window as a length

The concept: the same compound elutes a little earlier or later in each run. Alignment collects every sample's peaks on one RT axis and joins neighbours closer than the window. In the draft the window is a number in the rail, and the bands simply grow.

B recommended. Under the six traces sits one RT axis with every peak as a tick. Align slides a bracket the width of the window along it, in its own lane. A tick within the bracket's reach of the one before it joins that feature, and the band above grows with it. So "0.3 min" is a length the reader sees against the gaps. The hover highlights one peak (sample, RT, its shift) or one feature (its peaks in every row).

3 · Identification — the tolerance as a zoom, and the isomers as structures

The concept: a tighter mass tolerance leaves fewer formulas, down to one. That one formula is shared by two different molecules. In the draft, switching the tolerance swaps one ruler for another, and the two candidates are only names.

B and C recommended. B: changing Mass tolerance zooms the ruler 50× over 0.9 s: the 40 ticks spread, those outside leave at the edges, and the window narrows to the 5 nearest and then the 1. Hovering a tick names its formula and its error in ppm. C: the two candidate cards draw their skeletal structures: the same five carbons and four oxygens, a straight chain against a branch. This puts "same formula, different molecule" on the screen. The structures are drawn from the MAF's own SMILES strings.
