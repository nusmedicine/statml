Widget 99 shared-components · round 1 · how PLS is done, and N- vs P-integration

Your two questions on the draft: show how PLS is done, i.e. the matrix factorization, and does it help to illustrate N-integration and P-integration, or is that the next widget? What mixOmics actually does for one component (read from its source, internal_mint.block): it starts from the SVD of the cross-product X₁ᵀX₂, iterates w₁ ∝ X₁ᵀu, w₂ ∝ X₂ᵀt until the weights stop changing, then deflates X ← X − t cᵀ (c = Xᵀt / tᵀt) before the next component. The deflation is 01 cell 2's X ≈ T·Wᵀ, built one component at a time. Each section below draws one of these at the widget's 550px on the Concept blocks (simulated, the draft's), and §2 also on the lesson's mRNA–methylation pair. Blue-ruled: my recommendation; grey-ruled: the calls (§4).

1 · Finding the directions: the iteration, or the cross-covariance

A · Iterate. Start methylation's arrow anywhere (here straight up). Then, in turn: every mRNA gene's weight becomes its covariance with the methylation scores (the bars), which turns mRNA's arrow; every methylation site's weight becomes its covariance with the new mRNA scores, which turns methylation's arrow. Measured: from a poor start the arrows settle in 2–4 rounds on the Concept blocks (r 0.17 → 0.85 → 0.88) and in 2 on the lesson's blocks (0.874 → 0.889); from mixOmics' SVD start they are settled before the first round.

B · The cross-covariance. One table instead of a loop: the covariance of every mRNA feature with every methylation feature, X₁ᵀX₂ / n. Its first pair of singular vectors is (w₁, w₂), the arrows A settles on. On the lesson's blocks this table is 8,383 × 8,103.

Recommend A, as the PLS press on the Concept page: Start, then Update mRNA and Update methylation pressed in turn until r stops changing, with the covariance bars showing why each arrow turns. It shows the one idea that separates PLS from PCA: each block's weights are computed from the other block's scores. PCA keeps its single press. B is the same answer as a matrix and needs SVD, which the course has not taught.

2 · The matrix factorization: X ≈ t cᵀ + E, then component 2 from E

Concept blocks. Each block as its matrix: 80 samples (rows, sorted by subtype, bracketed in ink) × 2 features, coloured by value. Component 1 rebuilds it as a score column times a row of two numbers; what it misses is E, where component 2 is found the same way. PCA's E is smaller (it keeps the most variance); PLS's two score columns agree with each other, and line up with the subtypes.

The lesson's mRNA block. 405 × 8,383, drawn as an outline (the page shows derived numbers only). t: the 405 scores, sorted by subtype. wᵀ: the 8,383 gene weights, a row too long to read, with the five largest named. One component keeps 9.9% (PLS) or 11.2% (PCA) of the block; 03 cell 24 uses four.

Recommend a third page, Concept · Factorize · Data. Factorize: the Concept blocks as matrices, Method PCA · PLS, presses Score (t = Xw, the column), Rebuild (t cᵀ), Subtract (E = X − t cᵀ), and the formula card reads X ≈ T·Wᵀ in the lesson's letters. Data then gains the strip on the right of §2b for the chosen block: the score column and the weight row with its largest genes named.

3 · N-integration and P-integration (01 cell 2)

The lesson defines both by how the matrices line up, which is the factorization drawn twice. N: the same samples, different features, side by side; each block has its own weights, and the samples' scores are made to agree. P: the same features, different samples (studies), stacked; one set of weights serves every study. The lesson runs only N-integration (DIABLO); 40 batch-effect and 80 integration already handle samples from several batches with the same features.

Recommend A: this figure, at the top of the Factorize page, N drawn from the Concept blocks and P drawn as the contrast only, with nothing computed for it. It names what 99 does (N-integration) at the moment the student sees the two matrices side by side. A computed P example would teach a method the lesson never runs; 100 diablo keeps to N, as the lesson does.
