P3 · why three matrices: the same scores with q = k = x̃

Two measured facts. The embedding's own dot product puts each token's weight on itself (1.00 over the rows, here and in all four sentences), and it is symmetric: aspirin → chest equals chest → aspirin. With W_Q and W_K head 4 gives aspirin → chest 5.28 and chest → aspirin −1.49. The sentence that says why, two wordings for the section head

W1 · as a filter, the analogy you suggested

Each column of W is a trained set of 48 weights, applied to every token the same way a convolution kernel is applied at every position; each of a vector's 12 numbers is how strongly x̃ matches one column.

W2 · by role

Three trained matrices give each token a query, to score the other tokens with, a key, to be scored by, and a value, the numbers the weighted sum adds up. Scored against itself, the embedding gives every token its largest score with itself, and the same score in both directions.
