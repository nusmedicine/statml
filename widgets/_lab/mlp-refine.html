§1 · The activation, shown rather than named

The problem is real. The control reads Identity | ReLU | tanh — three words and no picture, and the notebook lists them as strings too. The one thing that makes them mean something is the SHAPE: a diagonal, a hinge, an S. All three below are drawn from the shipping ACTS, so the inset can never disagree with what the network computed.

A — inset in the network panel, at the hidden layer

σ acts AT the hidden units — but the hidden column owns the middle, so it goes in the free corner

~76px square in the free bottom-left corner; it costs no layout and fits at every k

B — its own small panel in the caption row

bigger and unmissable, but detached from the units it describes

roughly a third of the caption row's height, full legibility at lecture size

C — A, plus where this data's inputs actually land

the z values the hidden units really see, as a rug under the curve

C is the one with a second idea in it. The rug shows the z values the hidden units actually receive, and under ReLU everything left of 0 is flat — which is exactly where a dead unit lives. It connects the activation picture to the dead-unit caption the widget already prints, so the two teach one thing instead of two.

Measured while placing it, and it decides A against B. The inset cannot sit under the hidden column: the first draft put it there and it landed on the fourth node, and at k = 8 the column runs the panel's full height with no gap at all. In the free bottom-left corner — under the two input nodes, which only ever occupy the middle third — it fits at every k on the ladder. That is A's real cost: it is corner-constrained, so it cannot grow beyond about 90px. B can be any size, and pays for it by sitting away from the units.

On the animation. Switching activation should CROSSFADE the two curves, each with its own name fading with it — not morph one shape into the other. A morph would draw half-bent functions that are no activation at all and print a name that is false mid-frame, which is the lm-arc's rule (every printed number comes from the lerped model) read the other way. The crossfade is the SVM widget's own contour treatment.

§2 · The hover inspector — LIVE, move your pointer over the panels

Core already carries the channel (pointer: true) with one rule attached: an inspector must stay additive, because the lecture screen has no hover and a touch screen has none either. So nothing below may be the only route to anything.

Three things a pointer could inspect, in the order I would rank them: 1) a hidden unit — hover a node and its edges light up, its weights print, and its own straight piece draws on the boundary. That is the widget's subtitle claim ("one straight piece each") made pointable, and it is the strongest of the three by a distance. 2) the boundary — hover anywhere in the right panel and read P at that point, which turns the wash from decoration into a quantity. 3) an edge — hover a link and read its weight; the least valuable, since a number on a link is the thing you said students do not get, and thickness already carries it.

§3 · What I would ship, and what I would not

Inset C, in the network panel, crossfading on change — it answers the question you raised and carries the dead-unit idea for free.
Unit hover, yes — with the unit's line on the boundary panel. It is the one hover that teaches rather than reports.
Boundary hover, yes — cheap, and it makes the wash a quantity.
Edge hover, no — it prints the number the diagram exists to avoid, and every edge is already readable by thickness and colour.
And one addition the hover argues for: if the unit's line is worth seeing on hover, it is worth seeing without a pointer too. A Show each unit's line display toggle would put the same k lines on the boundary for a lecture screen, with hover as the fast path — which is the rule core states, honoured rather than worked around.
