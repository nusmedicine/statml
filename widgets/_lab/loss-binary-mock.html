loss-functions · one output and several, and binary two ways

Two asks from Kenneth, 2026-09-11: “can we show examples of 1 class and >1 class? also how best to explain that a binary class can be modeled as 1 label or 2 labels”. Both are extensions of the loss-functions draft, so every stage below is drawn in that widget's own form at its own 550 stage width, on the shared deep-learning geometry: PAD 14, GAP 22, a 200 × 150 curve panel, and a rows column of 300px.

The first ask is Kenneth's own figures. _lab/figs/dl-loss-mse.png and dl-loss-bce.png each draw two columns — one output ([batch], float32, headed 1 target and 1 label) beside several ([batch, N], float32, > 1 target and > 1 label) — while dl-loss-ce.png draws one column, [batch, N], float32 against [batch], long. The draft ships only the several-column of each. §1 puts the other column on a control.

The second ask is one measured fact. Softmax over two scores is the sigmoid of their difference, so a two-output model trained with CrossEntropyLoss and a one-output model trained with BCEWithLogitsLoss on z_B − z_A are the same model with the same loss. _lab/dl-loss-torch.py ran both over a drag of z_B from −2 to 4 and the two agree to 1.2e−07 at every point; the table in dl-loss-torch.txt §6 is where every number here comes from. §2 picks the device that shows it, §3 the rail that reaches it, §4 what its one derived bar is called and whether it drags.

Colour, as the draft sets it: the model's scores --c-group-a, every computed row --c-empirical, the target --c-reference, the held or dragged element and its point on the curve --c-highlight. Nothing here is random.
