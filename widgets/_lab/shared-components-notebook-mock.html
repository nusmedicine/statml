4 · P-integration: two studies stacked, one w

The same two genes measured in two studies of 40 samples each; study 2 reads higher on both genes (a study effect), and the studies' subtype mix differs (study 1 mostly subtype 1, study 2 mostly subtype 2). Stacked, X = [X₁; X₂] is 80 × 2, and one w serves both. As 01 cell 2 says, PLS here runs against a response: the subtype, so w ∝ Xᵀy, each gene's covariance with the subtype. Colour is the subtype; the study is the marker's shape (a circle or a square) and the row bracket, so colour carries one grouping. A: the stacked table centred as one. B: each study centred on its own first, which is what mixOmics' MINT does.

Recommend C: the notebook's description only, Stack · Find w, with the stacked table centred as one (A's drawing, without B). Measured here, the centring arm does not give a clean lesson: with the two studies' subtype mix different, A's scores track the subtype better (r 0.79) than B's (0.72), because part of A's score is the study (r 0.44 with study), and showing why that is worse needs a held-out study, which the lesson never runs. With an even mix the two arms differ by 0.03. So the page shows what 01 cell 2 says: the studies share the features, so they are stacked and share one w; each study's samples get their own scores. 40 batch-effect and 80 integration already cover removing a study effect.

5 · The notation: the notebook's W, or our c

01 cell 2 writes one matrix, W, for the loadings in X ≈ T·Wᵀ. The page uses w for the weights (t = Xw) and c for the loadings that rebuild X (mixOmics' deflation, c = Xᵀt / tᵀt). For the largest-variance direction c = w; for PLS they differ a little (mRNA kept 9.4% with w, 9.9% with c).

Recommend the notebook's single letter: rebuild with t wᵀ and call w the loadings, as 01 cell 2 and plotLoadings do; "kept" becomes the share of the block's variance along w. One letter fewer, and every symbol on the page is one the student has read.
