Your step back: pretraining is 84's (its Encoder page fills the lesson's [MASK]); this widget compares training from scratch with adapting a pretrained model, one way at a time, animated on the weights, and LoRA in detail. Every picture here is drawn from real matrices of 84's model (_lab/adapting-weights-measure.py, seed 0, 400 steps, the arc's rates).

What the measurement says a weight picture can show

Visible only as a change. Full fine-tuning moves each matrix by about a tenth of its size: on a picture of W itself nothing appears to happen. A picture of the change since training began, |Wt − W0|, shows it plainly, so every map below draws the change. Transfer changes the head and nothing else (the [CLS] vectors move 0%). LoRA changes only Q and V, and by more than full fine-tuning changes anything (its update is scaled by α/r = 4). The pattern turns during training on the Wrong drug task: at step 100 the change points only 45–48% of the way toward where it ends (cosine; 94–97% on Finding asserted), so an animation must play real snapshots, not fade in the final picture. Full fine-tuning's change is nearly low-rank: 80–96% of each Q or V change lies in 8 of 48 directions, the premise of LoRA; training from scratch shows the same (94–99%), so it is a property of short training as much as of fine-tuning, and the copy should say no more than the number.

3 · LoRA in detail: block 1's WQ
