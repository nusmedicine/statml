Slot 93 · ms-features · round 2: the map and its row

1 · Why only two retention times light up

  1. Each spot is one metabolite at its own m/z. The strong spots at RT 1.2–1.7 are lactic acid (m/z 89), malic acid (133), pyroglutamic acid (128), succinic acid (117) and citric acid (191). They are in other rows of the map.
  2. The trace is one row only: m/z 131.035 ± 5 ppm, a strip 0.0013 m/z tall. Only two metabolites have an ion at that m/z: glutaric acid and ethylmalonic acid, the isomer pair. So the trace has two peaks, at 2.17 and 3.44, and the other spots never enter it.
  3. The draft's box made it look otherwise. It was drawn 10 m/z tall (126–136), so it enclosed malic acid (133, RT 1.25) and pyroglutamic acid (128, RT 1.39). Those are two strong spots inside the box, absent from the trace. The box claimed more than the trace shows.
  4. The axes did not line up either. The map runs 0–10 min and the trace 1–4.5 min, so RT 2.17 sits at different x positions in the two panels (your arrows). A hover on one could not point at the same moment on the other.

Every frame below is drawn from the draft's engine, sample 932, after Integrate, at the widget's 610px. Each shows a hover at RT 3.44, to show how the two panels agree.

2 · Four ways to draw the row

B and D recommended together. B puts the map and the trace on one RT axis, the trace directly under the map, so a moment is one x position on both. The hover is a single vertical line through both panels and needs no syncing. The cost is that the map shows RT 0.8–5.6 min rather than 0–10: the two late eluters (phenyllactic acid at 7.9 and suberic acid at 8.9) leave the picture, and a note in the corner says so. The row is a line one m/z thin, not a box. D lets a click on any spot make its row the trace, and the page's m/z follows. Clicking lactic acid's spot gives one tall peak at RT 1.39 in this sample; clicking malic acid's gives one at 1.31. This answers "why only two" by letting the reader try another row. Detect and Integrate then work on whichever row is chosen. The Alignment and Identification pages stay on m/z 131.035, so the isomer story is unchanged. C, a draggable RT window over a 0–10 map, keeps the whole run but adds a gesture to learn and still leaves two x scales. It solves the zoom, and the zoom was not the confusion.

3 · Your calls

3.1 · The axes A · keep the 0–10 map and the zoomed trace, hover marked on both  |  B · one shared RT axis, 0.8–5.6 min (recommended)  |  C · a draggable RT window on a 0–10 map

3.2 · The row A · a line one m/z thin, not a 10-m/z box (recommended, with any of the above)

3.3 · Choosing the row D · a click on a spot makes its row the trace; a Row control lists the spots by name (recommended)  |  fixed at m/z 131.035
