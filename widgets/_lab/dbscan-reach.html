The hop is a cross-fade, and a reach ought to look like reaching

Two reports on the shipped build, and they are the same report. The dashed reach ring is thin and hard to see, and a hop lands rather than travels — the points arriving on a beat cross-fade into the cluster colour where they already sit, so nothing on screen moves outward and eps never looks like a distance. Everything below is drawn by the shipping engine at the real 550 px canvas and the real 4.6 px dot, on rings at the default eps 0.40.

The reach mark, at the mid-point of a hop

Each is the same frame — cluster 2, hop 5, halfway through — drawn seven ways. The ring is what a reader is supposed to follow, so what matters is whether it separates from the disc wash underneath it, which is itself grey and up to ten discs deep.

What each reach mark costs

Contrast is the mean channel distance between the ring's own pixels and the wash immediately beside it — measured on the figure, not judged. Under about 40 a mark is not separable at a glance; the shipped dashed ring is the row to beat. Ink is how many pixels the ring paints, which is what survives being shrunk to a lecture screen.

The hop itself — press one and watch

This is the part a still cannot settle. Four ways to spend the same beat, running side by side on the same clock. The question is whether a hop reads as the cluster travelling — and whether that survives Play, where a beat is 330 ms rather than the 700 ms one press of Step gets.
