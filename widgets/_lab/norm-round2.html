§1 · THE PICKER — normalize is a dropdown, transform is a button group

They are two steps of one pipeline and the rail's parallelism is half the argument (2.7), so two different shapes is a real inconsistency. It happened for a reason: five options do not fit a segmented group in a 250px rail — the segments are flex: 1, so each gets a fifth of the width whatever its label needs. The measured widths are printed under each candidate; a candidate whose longest label exceeds its segment share is marked.

What the measurements settle. B and E are out: five segments get 45px at the narrow rail and 55px at the wide one, and "Min–max" needs 65px at the button's own font. Shortening does not rescue it — 45px of button is about 29px of text, and "Min–max" is 49px. Cutting an option does not either: four segments get 56px. D fits — five tick labels at 13 / 51 / 98 / 153 / 200 with about 14px between them, comfortably inside 250px — but it is the wrong shape: 3.3 wants a choice slider for options that form a MAGNITUDE, and these do not. Their own scale-gap readings are 0.481 / 0.481 / 0.481 / 0.000 / 0.000 — a two-valued fact, not a ramp, so left-to-right would carry nothing.

Which leaves A and C, and the difference is what a dropdown hides. This widget's whole argument is that there is a SECOND operation; putting the transform's three options behind a dropdown hides that the choice exists at all, which is exactly the case 3.3 reserves segmented for. So the inconsistency is load-bearing rather than an oversight — five long-named options cannot be a button group at any rail width this layout reaches, and three short ones should not be a dropdown. My recommendation is A, unchanged, with this measurement recorded so it is not re-argued.

ASK 1. A (keep the split, now with a reason), C (both dropdowns, parallel but the transform's options hidden), or D (accept the slider despite 3.3)?

§3 · THE QUANTILE ACT — would an animation help?

Yes, and it is the only step in this widget a reader cannot picture. Min–max and z-score are one arithmetic line each; the log is a curve everyone has seen. Quantile normalisation is a three-step procedure over the whole table, and the notebook writes it out as prose (cell 16) precisely because it does not reduce to a formula.

The problem is scale: 1000 genes × 10 samples is not a countable thing (2.3). So the act needs its OWN small stage — 6 genes × 4 samples below, real numbers from the same gamma generator — behind a gate (3.4b), with the main figure untouched. Four steps, drawn as a filmstrip:

And the payoff is checkable on the small stage: after step 4 the four columns hold the same six values in a different order, so every sample's distribution is identical — which is the claim the main figure's boxplots make at 1000 genes and cannot show you arriving at.
