Widget 8, read by a biology MSc

The reader is a PHM5003 student: comfortable with gene counts, samples and means; not comfortable with log-likelihoods, 10⁻¹¹⁰, or the phrase "likelihood interval". They need one chain of intuition and nothing else:

I have counts → I guess a parameter → that guess says how likely each count was → multiply → one score for the guess → best score wins → and it is not exactly the truth, but more counts narrow the range.

Everything below is measured from the real widget at n = 60, seed 46, true size 2.5, mu 10 — the state in the screenshot that prompted this. Nothing here is built yet.

Every string in the widget, and whether it earns its place

The readout is the loudest offender but not the only one. Verdicts are a proposal, not a decision.

wherestringwhy
  1. Does variant C lose anything you would miss in a lecture? It drops every absolute likelihood and every log. What is left is a number you can point at and a range you can point at.
  2. Is a ratio really more readable than an exponent? That is variant B's whole bet: "1,500× more likely than a mean of 12" instead of "1.5 × 10⁻¹¹⁰". It is also more honest — a likelihood's absolute value is arbitrary and only ratios of it mean anything.
  3. The ratio does not stay plain, and variant B shows it. At n = 12 “beats Poisson by ×31,000” reads as a sentence; at n = 60 it is × 8.4 × 10²⁵ and back in scientific notation. Either cap the wording (“more than a billion times”) or accept that one number keeps an exponent — it is at least a ratio, so the exponent means something a reader can act on rather than being an arbitrary scale.
  4. Should the technical numbers be gone or one click away? A Show the arithmetic switch would keep them for you and for a student who wants them, without putting 10⁻¹¹⁰ in front of everyone.
  5. Does "a distribution would give 1" survive? It is the widget's stated misconception target, and it is the weakest thing on screen: two near-identical huge-negative exponents to compare. The clean half of the same idea — all spikes add to 1.000 on the top panel — costs nothing and reads instantly.
  6. Is the Likelihood / Log toggle for students or for you? The log axis is what optim maximises and the only one legible at n = 60. It is also a second axis convention to explain.
