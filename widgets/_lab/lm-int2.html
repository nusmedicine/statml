§1 · The equation card grows per-group rows — the notebook's rearrangement

The card keeps "the model" and "this model" (the printed lm output), and adds one row per line ON the graph, coloured to match. The reader sees the combined equation COLLAPSE into each group's own intercept and slope — under + the two rows differ only in intercept; under × they differ in both, and the men's slope (0.25) is visibly the sum 2.29 − 2.04. §2 · The 2×2 redrawn as an interaction plot — one grammar for both tabs

The x-axis holds the two diabetes positions; each sex is ONE LINE joining its two model predictions, exactly as on the age tab. The observed cell means are the fixed empirical ticks the model lines land on (or miss). Under + the lines are PARALLEL — both rise 10.18, the model's claim that diabetes does the same thing to everyone. Toggle × and the lines PIVOT about their left ends: women's steepens to +21.0, men's flattens to −0.02 — and the right-edge bracket names the nonparallelism: the difference of the two rises is 21.02, the printed interaction coefficient. The slope labels ride the lines and count during the ease, widget 29's rule.

+ independent — parallel lines

both lines rise exactly 10.18; the model has one diabetes effect. The observed means (ticks) disagree — the women's diabetic mean is missed low, the men's missed high

× interacting — the pivot

the lines land on the cell means: +21.0 for women, −0.0 for men; the bracket at right is the interaction, 21.02, as a difference of rises
