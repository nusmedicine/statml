Kenneth's plan for the round (2026-09-12, night) and one note he sent while it was being drawn. (1) the r² triangle on step 1 is clipped to the 250 kb clumping window; he wants the whole 100 × 100 half-matrix, unclipped. (2) a hover or indicator that ties the haplotype block to the triangle: a cell of the triangle lights the two SNPs' columns in the block and the rows where the two alleles travel together; a column of the block lights that SNP's row and column in the triangle; something stands in for the hover when there is no pointer. §1 · The whole triangle

Today the triangle stops 50 SNPs deep — the 250 kb the clumping can act on — on the mock's argument that the deeper half is r² ≈ 0. Kenneth wants the whole thing. A the full half-matrix, nothing on it; B the same with the clumping window's reach drawn across it as one dashed rule, so the reader can see that everything clumping will look at is above the line and that what is below it is ground. Both are drawn with the SNPs spaced at w / 100 so a triangle cell sits exactly above the midpoint of its two block columns — the shipped triangle spaces them at w / 99, which puts SNP 100 five pixels right of its own column; the build fixes that whichever is picked.

§2 · The link between the block and the triangle

Three stills at the widget's own 550px, then a live canvas. At rest, the causal SNP's row and column in the triangle are drawn as the same dashed reference rule its column in the block already wears, so the shape of "one SNP's pairs" — a V hanging from its position — is on screen before any pointer arrives. A column of the block under the pointer outlines that column and draws its V in the highlight colour, and the line under the block says what the V holds. A cell of the triangle under the pointer outlines the cell, draws its two legs up to the two SNPs on the axis, outlines both columns in the block, and bands the stretch between them in every row where the two alleles travel together — the rows whose pairing is one of the two commonest, which is what r² counts. The line under the block names the pair, the distance, the r² and the count of rows.

§3 · Hover, click, or both — the interaction model

Core offers two doors and they are different things. pointer: true hands draw() the pointer's position and repaints on movement — an inspector, nothing written, the figure exactly as before when the pointer leaves (hierarchical-clustering's distance matrix is the precedent). A regions table resolves a click to ONE parameter and writes it, so the URL carries it, a lesson link can open on it, and a hit-driven fingerprint state can pin it (t-sne, umap, fork-pipe-collider, lm-adjustment).

§4 · Step 3's axis — Kenneth's note on the flatline

Today's step 3 draws the forty strongest SNPs one a beat and then the other 120 as one batch on one beat: the strips become bars of totals and the sum is drawn as a single step across the wide column. The result he saw: the sum line runs level for a quarter of the panel and then drops to its end, which reads as "nothing happened for 120 SNPs" when the truth is 120 small steps. His proposal: rescale the x axis as SNPs are added, with a minimum window, so every SNP is drawn. Five ways, each drawn from the same person's 160 kept SNPs at the default (the person's score is 0.54; the sum stands at 0.89 after the fortieth): A today; B the axis is the kept count from the first frame, every SNP a column, no batch; C his — a window of W columns at unit width, and past W the axis grows one column a SNP so everything drawn so far squeezes left (W = 40, shown mid-run at 80 of 160); C′ the same with W = 150; D today's countable forty and totals bars, but the sum walks through the batch column as a compressed staircase of the 120 steps instead of one flat step.

The ends of the range, because the kept count runs from 0 to 1,000 (every SNP, at P ≤ 1): B at 35 kept (a 1,500-person base at P < 0.01, the round-one texture), B at 1,000, and C′ at 35, where a 150-column window leaves three quarters of the panel empty.

§6 · Step 2 over the triangle — clumping tied to the LD it acts on

Kenneth, after the round-3 review: "the clumping is hard to correlate with the LD". Today step 2 is the association plot alone and the arcs carry r² as opacity, so a reader has to remember step 1's triangle while looking at a different picture. The field's own figure for this is the regional plot with the LD triangle hanging under it, SNPs at the same x — and both panels here already share the region's coordinates. Proposed: step 2 keeps its plot and gains step 1's triangle beneath it, columns aligned (the plot's x axis is shifted half a SNP so position 5j kb sits over column j); while a clump is in flight the lead's row and column light in the triangle and the cells of the SNPs it absorbs are outlined — the same SNPs the arcs reach, said in the triangle's own terms; when the clump settles the triangle goes quiet and the axis ticks are the record. The haplotype block stays on step 1: folding it in too would put step 2 past 750px. Four stills of the default's first clump, then the finished run, then a live run.
