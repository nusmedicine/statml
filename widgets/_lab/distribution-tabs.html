Two tabs, and questions that name the distribution

Structure and wording for widget 11. The animations from the mechanisms page are unchanged and carry over. Tab 1 walks a live decision tree and ends on a nameplate; tab 2 turns each R function into the question it answers.

The two are told apart by their span, not by being lab values

Normal — adult height1st percentile 137 cm, 99th 183 cm1.3×

Log-normal — CRP1st percentile 0.27, 99th 27.8 mg/L105×

Every R distribution has the same four functions. The prefix is the question you are asking; the rest of the name is which distribution you are asking it about.

Judging questions

  1. Question 1. “Counting whole things” against “measuring on a scale” is the discrete/continuous fork in the words a student would use. Does it read that way, or does it need the words discrete and continuous in the answers themselves rather than only on the nameplate?
  2. Question 2. “Is there a maximum possible count?” replaces the old “did you decide up front how many times to look?”. It asks about the data rather than the procedure, and a ceiling is what actually separates the two count pairs.
  3. The nameplate. Is naming the distribution, its type, its parameters and its R call enough to stop the flow feeling abstract? Anything missing that a student would need when they meet the name again in a later lesson?
  4. Tab 2. Each card leads with the question, not the function. Does q's question (“how big is a bad-but-not-rare case?”) do a better job than “x at quantile q”?
  5. The d card on a continuous distribution is the only one whose answer is not a probability, and it says so in the card. Is that the right place for it?
