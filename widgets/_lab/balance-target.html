Widget 18 · the ideal-world line, and what the buttons do

Two questions in one page. First: is there a line worth drawing that says “here is where the model would cut if the classes were equally common”, so the four methods can be seen pushing towards it? Second: the widget has five controls and three buttons, and it should be obvious what each one does. The bottom of the page proposes cutting one control entirely.

Everything here is drawn with the widget’s own model.js, at the widget’s own 560 px panel width, so these are the real lines rather than an impression of them.

The measurement that says it is worth drawing

The target is the same logistic model fitted where the two classes are equally common — not the Bayes boundary, because these two clouds have unequal spreads so the Bayes rule is a curve, and drawing a curve against straight fitted lines would blame imbalance for the model being linear. Across six seeds it crosses x₂ = 5 within 0.07 of a 10-unit axis — four pixels — so it is a target and not a wobble.

Below: the share of a 6,000-patient population that each fitted line labels differently from the target, over 40 seeds. One number, in patients, comparable across methods — which recall and precision were not.

Three ways to show it

T1 · three lines, nothing else

before balancing · now · if the classes were equal. The cheapest version: the target is just a third line and the reader measures the gap by eye.

T2 · the gap as a band

Same three lines, and the plane between now and ideal washed. The band is the ground still to cover, and it shrinks to nothing as the plan runs — which is the thing being watched.

T3 · three lines and a gap bar

The gap as a length under the panel, on a fixed scale, with the unbalanced gap left behind it in grey. Costs 46 px of height and turns the comparison into a number you can read without arithmetic.

All four methods at once, at the current share

Not a proposal for the widget — the widget shows one method at a time and that is right. This row is here so the ordering can be judged: does undersampling really land further from the target than the other three, and is that visible or only in the number?

Which button does what — and one control to cut

The widget currently has three pages, and the middle one, One sample at a time, is not a different subject — it is the same plan at a slower pace. Core already has a word for that: its own tooltip for Step reads “advance one step, slowly, showing every stage”. So Step can be the slow motion and the page can go. Two pages, one fewer thing to learn, and the two drive buttons explain themselves.

  1. Page — which question. Balancing is what each method does to the data and where the model then cuts; Only the training set is what goes wrong if you balance before you split.
  2. Minority class — how rare the outcome is, from 80 of 200 down to 10 of 200. Everything else on the page is a response to this one.
  3. Balancing — what you do about it. The label on the Step button changes with it: Copy one sample, Drop one sample, Make one sample — and under None and Class weights it reads Nothing to change and greys out, because those two really do change no data.
  4. k — appears only under SMOTE, because only SMOTE has neighbours to choose from. Larger k reaches further out: at a 10% minority the share of new samples whose nearest real neighbour is a majority patient runs 17.8% at k = 1 to 35.8% at k = 9.
  5. Make one sample — one sample, slowly: the parent lights, its k neighbours light, one is chosen, the new point slides along that segment and lands.
  6. Play — the rest of them, fast, until the counts are even.
  7. Reset — back to the start. Note it returns every control to its default, not just the animation.
