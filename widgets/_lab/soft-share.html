How does one person show a share?

Widget 10's E-step gives every person a fraction of each curve. The shipped build encodes that fraction as a hue, ramping through grey at 0.5 — and the report on it was the one that matters: under hard cluster you can see the colours swapping; under share you cannot tell what changed. A hue that shifts a few percent per iteration is not a visible event, and the E-step is half the algorithm.

Drag the iteration slider and watch each panel. The question is not which is prettiest at rest. It is which one moves when the E-step runs, because that motion is the only thing on screen that says an E-step happened at all.

Published practice backs the split mark: Kern et al., The whole and its parts (Visual Informatics, 2024), encode mixture-component proportions with pie glyphs whose circular segments are the component densities. B and C are that idea at one point and at one column.

A · hue ramp through grey — what ships now

Colour is mix(ink3, side, |2s−1|). Hue says which curve, distance from grey says how sure.

B · split dot — each person is part amber, part blue

One circle per person, filled from the bottom by its share. A person at 0.55 is visibly 55% amber. The pie glyph, as a fill level.

C · stacked column — the weighted counts the M-step actually consumes

Each 5cm column is split at Σ share: the bottom is what the left curve is being fitted to, the top is what the right curve gets. The boundary dot is split. This is literally the M-step's input.

D · two overlaid dots, opacity = share

An amber dot at alpha = s over a blue dot at alpha = 1−s. No new mark geometry; the halo goes.
