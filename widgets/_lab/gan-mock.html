This is a one-dimensional GAN trained in the browser from the seed, exactly as c42's pseudocode: each round updates D on a batch of real and generated samples, then G on fresh noise with LG = −log D(G(z)). G and D are each a 1 → 16 → 1 network (tanh), Adam, batch 64, learning rate 0.002 for both. The canvas is drawn at the widget's width (540 px). training…

  • Seeds 1–40 (1–20 for the last row). Distance = the 1-D Wasserstein distance between 2,000 generated samples and the 1,000 real ones, after training; collapsed seeds are left out of the medians. Collapsed = under 10% or over 90% of the generated samples on one side of 0.
  • The one bell at equal rates fails in a way that would confuse a class: D goes flat at ½ while G sits off the data (median distance 0.64), so the figure would say “D cannot tell” about samples that are visibly wrong. The two modes at equal rates cover both modes on every seed, under either schedule.
  • D never reaches ½ between the modes. G maps noise to x continuously, so it cannot jump the gap: a thin bridge of generated samples stays between the modes, the data have almost none there, and D reads 0.07–0.24 at x = 0. That is the optimal discriminator pdata/(pdata + pG) where pdata ≈ 0. So the claim is D(x) = ½ where the data are, not everywhere.
  • The default seed below, 4, is among the 13 (A) and 19 (B) seeds of 40 that end within 0.17 of the data with D within 0.06 of ½ at both modes; 5, 7, 10 and 13 are in both lists too. Seed 4 sends every generated sample left of 0 early on (rounds 25–100 under A, 1–4 under B) and recovers.
  • Training takes ~215 ms in node, once per seed, in compute. The figure only replays snapshots.

The calls before building

  
1. The real dataTwo modes, ½N(−2, 0.5²) + ½N(2, 0.5²), 1,000 draws (shown); or one bell.

  
Recommend two modes: at the pseudocode's own schedule it covers both modes on all 40 seeds and ends near the data (median 0.18), where one bell ends off the data with D flat at ½ (median 0.64).


  
2. The pressesA: the pseudocode's schedule (1 D step, 1 G step a round); Step advances 25 rounds, Train plays all 3,000. B: 20 D steps then 20 G steps a round, one press whose label alternates Update D / Update G, so each half is seen alone; Train plays the 150 rounds. Switch with the mock-only schedule above and press Step a few times on each. The two schedules end alike (table).

  
Recommend B. It is c42's two halves as two presses: Update D raises D over the data and lowers it over the generated samples; Update G then slides the generated samples along D's slope (the arrows), with G never touching the data. Under A both move at once in every frame. B is the pseudocode with each update repeated 20 times, which the card says. Its cost: it is not quite the loop the notebook prints.


  
3. The noise-to-x lines21 lines from evenly spaced quantiles of z ~ N(0, 1) (the lower axis) up to G(z) (shown, as in Goodfellow et al. 2014's Figure 1); or no lines.

  
Recommend the lines: they show what G is, a map from noise to x, and where the lines bunch is where the generated samples pile up.


  
4. The gradient arrowson 21 generated samples, the direction and size of the step Update G gives each one, (1 − D)·dlogit D/dx (shown, --c-slope); or none.

  
Recommend the arrows: they are the generator's only information about the data, which is c42's first bullet.


  
5. The loss panelLD and LG per round with their values at D = ½ marked (2 ln 2 = 1.39 and ln 2 = 0.69) (shown); or no loss panel.

  
Recommend the panel: it shows that neither loss goes to zero. Both settle at the value D = ½ gives, which is how a GAN's losses differ from every loss the class has trained before.


  
6. The coloursreal data --c-reference (the fixed target), generated samples and G's loss --c-empirical (what is built), D(x) and D's loss --c-theory, arrows --c-slope (shown); or real and generated as --c-group-a / --c-group-b.

  
Recommend as shown: real is the benchmark the moving thing is judged against, which is --c-reference's role; D's line and D's loss share a colour, as do G's samples and G's loss.

Recommend not shown: one page, the base GAN, as picked. Seed 4 sends every generated sample to the left mode early on and recovers, which Train shows in passing.
