1 · Matching: two places a tween can teach

The comparison press. Now it fades every cosine in at once (0.5 s), so nothing shows that a cosine is one signature laid against one reference. Scan: the chosen signature's row fills one reference at a time, left to right, and the panel below lays each reference under the signature as its cell fills; then the other rows fill, one after another; then each row's best is outlined and the panel settles on the best match and the runner-up (2.9 s). A click on another row. Now the panel below redraws at once. Ease: its three profiles change height into the new signature's and its matches' (0.6 s), and a mark beside the row labels moves from the old row to the new, so the reader sees the heatmap row and the panel are one choice. 2 · Catalogue: transitions and transversions

Your lesson's figure (cell 3) is the standard square: the purines A and G on top, the pyrimidines C and T below, transitions along the top and bottom (A↔G, C↔T), transversions down the sides and across. It colours transitions blue and transversions red, and here blue is C>A and red is C>T, so these draw the same square in the class colours: every arrow is one written change, in the colour of the class it is read as. Folding: the twelve written changes are twelve arrows, the six from a purine paler, like their bars; on Read from the pyrimidine the six pale ones fade as their bars fold into their partners', leaving the six the catalogue counts, every one starting at C or T, and Ti and Tv label the two kinds. Fixed: the six arrows and their labels from the start. Card above: the same square with a sentence, above the figure, where pages 2 and 3 have their formula; the bars keep the full width.
