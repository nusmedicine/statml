processing-layers · the design picks, drawn to scale

Seven picks for the processing-layers widget — five layer pages from PHM5005 05-3, cells 1–28: Linear, Convolutional (with transposed), Recurrent, Attention, Graph. Every stage below is drawn at the real 550 stage width, at the geometry constants widgets/tensors/main.js uses (PAD 14, GAP 22, operator 26, a signed two-decimal float cell 46 × 26, an integer cell 30 × 26, an image pixel 14 square). Every number comes from the seeded makeRng, with the weights drawn from the bounds PyTorch's default initialiser uses; the prints are torchPrint, the same function the tensors widget prints with. The width each band leaves at 550 is printed under it.

Colour: the input is --c-group-a, the second operand (weights, kernel, keys) --c-group-b, every result --c-empirical, the element being computed and the cells it reads --c-highlight. The feature maps and the reconstruction carry a signed ramp, --c-value-low below the bias to --c-value-high above it, because at an untrained initialisation a filter's whole output can sit on one side of its bias.

§1 · The rail at the real 300px

A one row of five, 60px a button. B a two-column grid, 150px a button, with Graph on a full row (w-seg-grid and w-seg-btn--span, both already in controls.js). Both are the shell's own DOM and the shell's own CSS, so what truncates here truncates in the widget. Under them, the whole rail for the Convolutional page — layer, the four convolution controls, Play speed and the drive block — beside the shortest stage in the widget, so the height the rail spends can be read against the height the figure spends.

§2 · The Linear page, and the value cell it fixes

The reference page: cell 5's Linear(4, 3) on randn(2, 4). Two bands — the operands, then the result with its print. One output element is lit with the whole row of x and the whole row of W it reads, at once. A two decimals in a 46 × 26 cell with the exact print under. B one decimal in a 40 × 26 cell.

§3 · The Convolutional stage

Cell 8's 16 × 16 image with a 6 × 6 square at rows and columns 5–10, through cell 9's Conv2d(1, 2, 3, stride=2, padding=1). A one row: input, kernel, feature maps. B two bands: the padded image with the window frame over the two feature maps, then the arithmetic for one position. Under them, what stride = 1 does to band 1, and the same two settings at the 770 stage, which is the one page where the two frames give different pixels.

§4 · The transposed reconstruction and the True image reveal

Cell 14's ConvTranspose2d(2, 1, 3, stride=2, padding=1, output_padding=1) on the two feature maps band 1 above just produced. Then the reveal, both options at the same seed: A the reconstruction alone, B the same footprint redrawn as the signed difference z − img.

§5 · The Recurrent stage

Cell 19's [2, 5, 4] through a bidirectional recurrence with 3 hidden features, in the row order of dl-layer-rnn-bi.png: Forward · Input · Reverse · Output. The recurrence drawn is h_t = tanh(W_x x_t + W_h h_{t−1} + b) at the LSTM initialiser's bounds — the page draws f closed, so the values stand in for whichever of RNN, LSTM and GRU is named. A all four rows at both settings. B the Reverse and Output rows follow direction.

§6 · The Attention stage, at both projections

Cell 22's three token embeddings through MultiheadAttention(embed_dim=4, num_heads=1). Band 1 puts X above with Q and K beside each other below it, then the scores and the softmax weights as a heat grid; band 2 is dl-layer-attention-qkv.png's layout for one query. Drawn twice: at the notebook's own random projections, and at identity projections.

§7 · The Graph stage

Cell 27's four nodes on the chain 0–1–2–3, three features each, through GCNConv(3, 3). Three bands, as dl-layer-gnn.png has them: Input · Aggregate · Transform. A horizontal shaded strips at a 30px cell, the orientation his figure uses, with the values in the readout and the aggregation coefficients printed on the arcs. B vertical strips at the 46px numbered float cell.
