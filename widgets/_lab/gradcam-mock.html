§1 · The task the net is trained on

What the widget needs from it. Three things at once: the net learns the task without a shortcut (clean accuracy high at watermark 0 %); the planted mark takes over when it is on every image of one class (clean accuracy at chance at 100 %); and the heatmap of a model WITHOUT the shortcut lands on the object — for BOTH classes, since the reader picks the test image and half of them are the other class. The last is where the cell fails, and the reason is Grad-CAM's own arithmetic: with two classes and a global-average head, the two rows of the linear layer are near-opposites, so a class that is defined by the ABSENCE of a feature (no nucleus; no granules) has a heatmap that is ReLU of the negative of the other's — empty where the evidence is, and scattered elsewhere.

§2 · The figure

Both are drawn at 550, the width the stage is reviewed at, on the pick's model at watermark 0 %, a cell test image, the layer conv2 (cell 137's layer2), the predicted class, every step shown. A puts your Grad-CAM diagram under the gallery 61 shipped: the top band is the whole network on this image with the chosen layer's column marked, and the bottom band is the method, left to right in its own order — the maps, their gradients, the averages, the weighted sum, ReLU, the heatmap on the image. B is your diagram alone, laid out as you drew it: forward along the top, backward along the bottom.

§3 · The drive: one Step through the method

The widget opens on the image and the empty network. Each press is one of the notebook's numbered steps, and the button says which it is about to do (4.4b, widget 60's device): Forward pass — the maps fill and the score prints; Gradients — ∂yc/∂Ak lands beside every map; Average — each gradient map collapses to its αk; Weighted sum — Σ αk Ak, signed; ReLU, upsample — the negative part goes and the rest is drawn on the image. Play runs the five and stops. The band below is shown after each press; the gallery above it does not change after the first.

§4 · The evidence: the caveat drawn, not said

The notebook's caveat is correlation with decision-making, not causal reasoning, and the planning measurement found the picture that shows it: a bright mark lights the fine layer's heatmap WHETHER OR NOT the model uses it, because the CAM is weighted by the activations. So the widget must not let a hot corner on a watermarked image stand as proof of shortcut learning. The honest pairing is the heatmap on a CLEAN image beside the accuracy on clean images. Two ways to put that on the stage:

A. An Image control, Clean · Watermarked, that swaps the test image the whole figure is about; the readout always prints both accuracies. B. A third band that always shows both readings at the chosen layer — the same test image clean and watermarked, each with its heatmap and its prediction — with the two accuracies and the heat shares under them. §5 · The rail

The spec as it would be written, rendered by buildControls at 300px. Two sections: what the training data is (the watermark's rate, the seed — both DATA, each retrains), and what is read off the trained model (the layer, the class, the test image, clean or watermarked — all DISPLAY, none retrains and none resets the steps). The heat shares and the two accuracies are the readout under the drive row.
