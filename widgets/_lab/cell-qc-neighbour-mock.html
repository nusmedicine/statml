cell-qc · the neighbourhood, without looking like a droplet

What you saw is what it draws. A circle with dots inside it is a container, and in this widget the container a reader has in mind is a droplet — so the panel says “here is a droplet with cells in it” before it says anything else. Nothing in it marks the focal droplet as the subject, and nothing shows that the other dots are neighbours of it. Both are fixable, and your spider is one of the fixes.

1 · “neighbors based on distance from something?” — yes, and here is the something

Every droplet is a point whose coordinates are what fraction of its transcripts came from each cell type's genes. A hepatocyte sits near the hepatocyte corner; an immune cell near the immune corner; a droplet holding one of each sits between them, because its transcripts really are a mixture. Distance is difference in that mixture — two droplets are near when what was sequenced in them was similar.

The made-up doublets are points in the same space, made by adding two real droplets' transcripts together. So the question “what share of your nearest neighbours are made up” is really: of the things whose transcript mixture is most like yours, how many are mixtures I built on purpose? That is the whole method, and it is why no cut-off on a droplet's own counts can do it.
