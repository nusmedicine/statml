Slot 4 · the distance matrix — does it earn its space, and on which tab?

Two questions from review. "Students get confused about whether we are clustering rows or columns" — and the widget currently skips the step where that distinction is made. The notebook's pipeline is data → dist() → hclust() → cutree(); the widget draws the data, the tree and the cut, and never shows the dist() in between. Everything below is drawn from the shipping model.js.

The rows/columns confusion is a fact about the distance matrix, not about the tree. Clustering the rows of a 50 × 20 matrix means computing a 50 × 50 matrix of gene-to-gene distances; clustering the columns means a 20 × 20 matrix of sample-to-sample distances. Those two objects are different sizes and are visibly different pictures — which is why showing them may settle the question that words about it do not.

§1 · THE CLUSTER TAB — 20 points, and the 20 × 20 they produce

The distance matrix is the thing the linkage and the cut both read, and it is the only place the Distance control acts directly. Rows and columns are the same twenty points, so it is symmetric with a blank diagonal — which is itself worth a student seeing once.

ASK 1. On the Cluster tab: (a) no distance matrix, as now; (b) a small one beside the scatter, ordered by the tree so the blocks line up with the dendrogram; (c) ordered by point index, so it is the raw dist() output before any clustering has happened. (c) shows the input, (b) shows the result — and only (c) makes "the tree re-orders it" a thing you can watch.

§2 · THE HEATMAP TAB — the two distance matrices, side by side

This is the rows-versus-columns question made concrete: the same 50 × 20 matrix produces two different distance matrices, and each one feeds its own tree. Sizes below are the real ones.

ASK 2. On the Heatmap tab: (a) nothing, as now; (b) both distance matrices small, under their own trees, permanently; (c) one at a time, following a "clustering the ___" control the reader sets to rows or columns. (c) costs a control but answers the question by making the reader choose — they cannot ask "which are we clustering?" of a widget that has just asked them.

§3 · DOES THE DISTANCE CONTROL VISIBLY CHANGE IT?

If Euclidean and Manhattan produce indistinguishable pictures the matrix is decoration. Same points, the two metrics, each scaled to its own range.

§4 · THE SAME DIAL ON BOTH TABS — what the matrix does as the effect goes

node widgets/_lab/hc-lift.mjs prints this. The Cluster tab has a dial for how much structure is really there and the Heatmap tab does not, so the second tab only ever shows the method working. The matrix takes the same dial: the four planted blocks dissolve, the columns stop recovering the conditions — and the cut goes on returning exactly the number of groups it was asked for.

ASK 3. Give the Heatmap tab the same Separation control (renamed to cover both — "how much structure is really there"), so both tabs can fail? Every other tabbed widget in this repo shares its data controls across views — matrix-factorization shares programmes and seed, odds-and-risk shares the whole 2 × 2. I recommend yes.
