Widget 26 · fork-pipe-collider — three treatments of "adjust"

The widget's one interaction is Adjust for Z: Off / On, flipping between the notebook's own regression pairs. The design question is what that flip shows. Three candidates, drawn on the real data (causal-model.js, verbatim from 06-02, seed 1, n = 1000, the 550 px canvas of the side layout): A two slopes on the scatter, B the scatter coloured by the third variable with per-stratum lines — "adjusting" as a picture of comparing like with like — and C a coefficient forest, closest to the notebook's own tables. The fork row is the hard case (Z = age, continuous); the collider row is the clean one (Z = ICU, binary). Whatever wins must work for both.

The n ruling is already measured (causal-measure.mjs, 200 seeds): the fork's adjusted arm (+0.1, SE ≈ 1/√n) is significant only 32% of the time at n = 200 and 87% at n = 1000 — so n stays at the notebook's 1000 and gets no control; a smaller, prettier n would teach "adjusting made it go away". The R² trap is confirmed: the WRONG model fits better in both directions (pipe 0.95 → 0.99, collider 0.00 → 0.22).

Round two — how to SHOW the adjustment (Kenneth: "the colors do what here?")

The colour tint says where the third variable sits, but not why removing it moves the slope. The obvious bridge — thin fit lines inside each colour band — was measured and killed: within quartile bands the fork's slope is still −0.38 to −0.30 (the band is too wide to de-confound a 15-sd age), and the pipe's is 5–9 against an adjusted −0.04. Drawing them would teach the OPPOSITE of the adjusted fit. The added-variable view cannot lie: regress both axes on the third variable and plot what is left, and the residual cloud's slope IS the adjusted coefficient (Frisch–Waugh; 0.110 / −0.040 / −0.180 on this seed, to the digit). And the tint answers for itself: in the residual view the colours stop running anywhere — the visible proof that the third variable has been taken out. The widget would tween each patient from the data view to the residual view.

The interaction is the open choice: (a) a View control — Data / Third variable removed — shown once the model is fitted, with the dots sliding between views; or (b) tie the slide to Adjust itself, so switching the model moves the patients. (a) keeps the two ideas separate (what the model is; where its number comes from) and keeps the swing-of-the-line ease meaningful; (b) is one control fewer but conflates the two moves. Round two proposes (a).
