tensors · drawing a three-dimensional tensor without hiding cells

The shipped draft stacks one grid per sample with a whole-cell offset (candidate A). With two samples the back grid shows its top row and right column and hides the other four cells; with three samples the middle grid shows two cells of ten. The reference drawings — the TensorFlow guide's three pictures of a [3, 2, 5] tensor (side-by-side blocks, an offset stack, a solid cube), NumPy's "a set of tables, stacked as though printed on separate pages", Chollet's "cube of numbers" — all trade the same two things: how much depth the picture suggests against how many cells it shows. Every candidate below is drawn twice, for [2, 2, 5] and for [3, 2, 5], and the caption counts the hidden cells.

§1 · Five drawings of the same tensor

A the current stack, whole-cell offset. B the exploded stack: the same slabs spread along the depth diagonal far enough that none overlaps, joined by the dim-0 arrow — the TensorFlow "front" picture. C the exploded stack laid flat: slabs side by side, each in a frame labelled by its dim-0 index, the three dimension arrows drawn once — the TensorFlow "numpy" picture, and what NumPy and PyTorch print. D C with the indices written on the edges: dim-0 index on the frame, dim-1 index down the left, dim-2 index across the top, so T[1, 0, 2] = 13 reads off the figure without hovering. E the solid cube, values on the front face only, shown to be rejected.

§2 · Rank 4 — the lesson's image batch, [3, 3, 2, 5]: sample, channel, height, width

Ninety cells, numbered 1–90 in reading order, at the 550px stage width. D4 frames within frames: one frame per sample down the page, inside it one frame per channel across, inside that the 2 × 5 grid of pixels; the edge indices on the first grid only. B4 your exploded stack: one stack per sample across the page, the three channels spread up the diagonal, nothing overlapping. A4 your overlapped stack, as the lesson's 4D figure draws it, for the count of what it hides. A fifth dimension is one more frame round D4 (a row of them) or one more row of B4 — the lesson's 5D figure is B4 repeated.

What the references settle. The TensorFlow guide draws the same rank-3 tensor three ways and says outright that there are many ways to draw one; its rank-4 example is drawn as blocks of blocks, not as a cube. NumPy's own mental model is that the LAST index is the column and the second-to-last the row, which is what C and D make literal: the last two dimensions are the grid you can read, and every earlier dimension is a frame round grids. The named-tensor argument (Rush, "Tensor Considered Harmful") is that what a reader needs is the NAME of each dimension, not its position — which the widget already does with "0 sample", "1 sequence", "2 feature", and D extends to the indices along each.
